Dual Citizenship

Guyana is one of the most open jurisdictions in South America for dual citizenship. The Constitution of the Co-operative Republic of Guyana and the Citizenship Act together permit citizens to hold nationality in more than one state simultaneously.

Notably, Guyana had historically restricted dual citizenship, particularly for persons who voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality. This changed significantly with constitutional amendments that recognized the rights of the large Guyanese diaspora — estimated at over one million people living in the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom — to maintain ties with Guyana.

There is no requirement to renounce prior nationality upon naturalizing as a Guyanese citizen, and Guyanese nationals who naturalize elsewhere do not automatically forfeit their Guyanese citizenship. This makes Guyana an accessible second citizenship for those in the Commonwealth diaspora or investors looking at South America’s fastest-growing economy.

Certain constitutional offices (President, Prime Minister) may require exclusive Guyanese citizenship or impose restrictions on dual nationals, but these are electoral and public office restrictions, not a general prohibition on holding dual citizenship.

Acquisition of Nationality

Guyanese nationality is governed by the Constitution of Guyana (1980, as amended) and the Guyana Citizenship Act. The legal framework reflects Guyana’s identity as a Commonwealth member with strong ties to Caribbean and South American legal traditions.

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Guyana applies unrestricted jus soli. Any person born on the territory of Guyana acquires Guyanese citizenship at birth, regardless of the nationality or immigration status of the parents.

This is one of the most expansive birthright citizenship rules in South America, and it mirrors the rule applied by many Caribbean Commonwealth states. The only traditional exception is children born to foreign diplomats in official service, which is typically excluded by customary international law.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

A person born outside Guyana acquires citizenship by descent if at least one parent is a Guyanese citizen at the time of the child’s birth. Key details:

  • Citizenship by descent is available in the first generation born abroad. Subsequent generations born abroad generally require registration or naturalization unless the foreign-born child themselves registers the descent citizenship before having children.
  • Registration of births of Guyanese citizens born abroad should be made at the nearest Guyanese High Commission or Embassy.
  • Children born outside of marriage may claim citizenship through the mother automatically; through the father upon establishment of legal paternity.

By Adoption

A child legally adopted by a Guyanese citizen may be registered as a citizen. The adoption must be recognized under Guyanese law and the application submitted to the Ministry of Home Affairs. The child must typically be under 18 at the time of adoption.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Guyanese citizen does not confer automatic citizenship. The spousal route is:

  • 2 years of legal residence in Guyana following a recognized marriage to a Guyanese citizen.
  • The marriage must be legally recognized in Guyana and subsisting at the time of application.
  • Subject to standard character and domicile requirements.

The 2-year spousal track is notably shorter than most Commonwealth comparators, reflecting Guyana’s policy of supporting family reunification.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Naturalization is the primary route for most foreign nationals. Administered by the Ministry of Home Affairs in Georgetown.

Standard Requirements

  • 5 years of lawful residence in Guyana immediately before application.
  • During the 5 years, absences must be reasonable and not indicate abandonment of Guyana as primary residence.
  • Must be of good character — police clearance from Guyana and country of origin required.
  • Must demonstrate intention to remain domiciled in Guyana.
  • English proficiency is expected (Guyana’s only official language) but not formally tested.
  • Two Guyanese citizen references.
  • Medical certificate.
  • Application fee.

Reduced Residency Categories

  • Commonwealth citizens: Eligible for naturalization after 1 year of lawful residence. Guyana’s Commonwealth membership creates this accelerated pathway, identical to Belize’s rule, and applies to citizens of all 56 Commonwealth member states.
  • Spouses of Guyanese citizens: 2 years (see Marriage section).
  • CARICOM nationals: CARICOM free movement rights apply, facilitating establishment of lawful residence.
  • Persons of Guyanese descent: May be eligible for faster registration or citizenship resumption pathways.

Preferred Pathways

For Commonwealth citizens: The 1-year naturalization track is the headline feature. A UK, Canadian, or Indian national who establishes residence in Guyana — increasingly attractive given the booming oil economy — can apply for citizenship after just 12 months.

For investors and business migrants: Guyana’s extraordinary economic growth (GDP growth of 20%+ annually since 2020) has created demand for business-ready residents. While there is no formal investor visa, establishing a company and obtaining a work permit starts the clock on the 5-year (or 1-year for Commonwealth) naturalization timeline. The government has signaled interest in developing more structured investment migration pathways.

For CARICOM nationals: The free movement framework allows skilled CARICOM workers to live and work in Guyana without a work permit, providing a natural residency base.

For the diaspora: Guyanese nationals who previously naturalized abroad (particularly in the US, UK, and Canada) and their children can use descent registration or simplified pathways to maintain or recover citizenship.

Special Clauses & Programs

The Oil Economy Opportunity

Guyana’s ExxonMobil-led oil discovery in the Stabroek Block (announced 2015, production from 2019) transformed the country into one of the world’s fastest-growing economies. The Sovereign Wealth Fund (Natural Resource Fund) was established to manage oil revenues. Key implications for prospective residents and investors:

  • GDP per capita has surged dramatically — Guyana is now among the richest countries in South America per capita on a PPP basis.
  • The government is investing heavily in infrastructure, housing, education, and health.
  • Demand for skilled workers, professionals, and entrepreneurs is extremely high.
  • Georgetown is undergoing significant urban development.

Commonwealth Advantage

As a full Commonwealth member, Guyana benefits from:

  • 1-year naturalization track for Commonwealth citizens.
  • Commonwealth scholarship and educational programs.
  • Simplified diplomatic relationships with 55 other Commonwealth states.
  • British-derived common law system — familiar legal framework for UK, Canadian, and Australian nationals.

CARICOM Full Member

Guyana is a founding member and major player in CARICOM. Free movement rights for CARICOM skilled nationals apply, and Guyana participates in CARICOM’s Single Market and Economy (CSME). This is significant given Guyana’s position as one of the most economically dynamic CARICOM states.

Diaspora Engagement

The Guyanese government has increasingly focused on engaging the million-plus diaspora in North America and the UK. Dual citizenship recognition is central to this policy, allowing diaspora members to invest in and return to Guyana without surrendering their acquired foreign nationalities.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Guyana does not currently operate a formal Citizenship by Investment program. Unlike some Caribbean neighbors, there is no published investment threshold for expedited citizenship.

Given the rapidly growing economy and increasing international interest, there have been discussions within government circles about developing structured investment migration programs, but as of 2026, no formal CBI program exists.

Residency by Investment (RBI)

There is no formal Residency by Investment program either. However, the Guyana Investment Agency (Go-Invest) facilitates investment in key sectors (oil and gas services, agriculture, tourism, manufacturing, ICT) and assists with work permits and residency documentation.

Significant investors who establish enterprises and employ Guyanese workers are typically well-regarded by immigration authorities, and their residency applications receive favorable treatment in practice, even without a formal program.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Company Formation

  • Private Company Limited by Shares: Registered under the Companies Act 1991 with the Deeds Registry and the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA). Straightforward incorporation process.
  • Foreign Company Branch: International companies can register a branch in Guyana.
  • Sole Proprietorship / Partnership: Available for smaller operations.

Guyana’s business environment has improved significantly since the oil boom. The government has streamlined registration procedures and created incentives to attract foreign direct investment.

Entrepreneur & Startup Visas

No dedicated startup visa exists. Foreign entrepreneurs must obtain a work permit to work in their own company. The process involves:

  1. Registering the business with the Deeds Registry.
  2. Applying for a work permit through the Ministry of Home Affairs.
  3. Demonstrating that the business provides economic benefit to Guyana.

The Ministry has been flexible with entrepreneurial applications in the current growth environment.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

The Bank of Guyana is the central bank. Major commercial banks include:

  • Republic Bank (Guyana) Limited (Trinidad-based; largest in Guyana)
  • Demerara Bank
  • Citizens Bank Guyana
  • Bank of Baroda (India; serves Indo-Guyanese community)
  • Scotiabank Guyana

Banking infrastructure is developing rapidly. International wire transfers are possible but AML due diligence is thorough. Guyana uses the Guyana Dollar (GYD); USD is widely accepted in commercial transactions.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Go-Invest: Guyana Office for Investment — provides information, facilitation, and concierge services for foreign investors. Liaison with government agencies.
  • Petroleum sector incentives: Production Sharing Agreements with favorable terms; import duty exemptions on equipment.
  • Agriculture incentives: Tax holidays for agro-processing; duty exemptions on inputs. Guyana is a major rice and sugar producer.
  • Tourism: Eco-tourism incentives; Guyana’s Amazon interior is a largely untapped eco-tourism destination.
  • Free Trade Zones: Guyana is developing free zone infrastructure in Georgetown.
  • CARICOM trade preferences: Duty-free access to the CARICOM market of ~18 million people.

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A Guyanese citizen of full age may renounce their citizenship by making a formal declaration to the Ministry of Home Affairs. The renunciation is registered and takes effect on the date of registration. A renouncing citizen must typically have, or be in the process of acquiring, another nationality to avoid statelessness.

Involuntary Loss

  • Fraud: Citizenship obtained through fraudulent misrepresentation or concealment of material facts may be revoked by the Minister.
  • Disloyalty: A naturalized citizen who acts in a manner seriously prejudicial to Guyana’s interests, voluntarily serves in the armed forces of a state at war with Guyana, or assists a foreign government in a hostile act against Guyana may have citizenship revoked.
  • Citizens by birth are generally protected from involuntary loss except in narrow fraud-related circumstances.

Importantly, Guyana does not revoke citizenship for acquiring another nationality, consistent with its full dual citizenship stance.

Recovery / Resumption

Persons who previously lost Guyanese citizenship — whether through former prohibitions on dual nationality or through renunciation — may apply for resumption. Applications are considered by the Ministry of Home Affairs. Diaspora members with strong family and economic ties to Guyana are typically viewed favorably.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For naturalization:

  • Valid passport (all relevant passports showing entry and exit stamps)
  • Birth certificate
  • Police clearance from Guyana and country of origin
  • Proof of lawful status in Guyana (residence permit, work permit, entry stamps)
  • Proof of address (utility bills, lease)
  • Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  • Two character references from Guyanese citizens of standing
  • Recent passport photographs
  • Completed application form (obtained from Ministry of Home Affairs)
  • Application fee

For citizenship by descent:

  • Applicant’s birth certificate
  • Guyanese parent’s birth certificate and citizenship documentation
  • Proof of parent’s Guyanese citizenship at time of applicant’s birth

Where to Submit

Primary office: Ministry of Home Affairs 6 Brickdam and Vlissengen Road Georgetown, Guyana

Applications can also be initiated at Guyanese High Commissions and Embassies in the UK, USA, Canada, and other countries with significant Guyanese communities.

Processing Time

  • Standard naturalization: 6–18 months
  • Commonwealth 1-year track: 6–12 months after the qualifying residence year
  • Citizenship by descent registration: 3–6 months

Processing times are affected by staff capacity and application volume. Political changes can also impact turnaround times.

Fees

  • Naturalization application: approximately GYD 5,000–20,000 (~USD 25–100) — fees are low by regional standards
  • Citizenship by descent: similar range
  • Certificate of citizenship: small administrative fee

Confirm current fees with the Ministry of Home Affairs or nearest Guyanese mission, as these are subject to change.

Related Countries

  • Suriname — eastern neighbor; shares border and Amazon rainforest; former Dutch colony to Guyana’s former British colony
  • Brazil — southern neighbor; largest country in South America; significant cross-border trade
  • Venezuela — western neighbor; ongoing border dispute over Essequibo region (major territorial claim)
  • Trinidad and Tobago — CARICOM partner; close economic and cultural ties; large Guyanese diaspora
  • United Kingdom — former colonial power; large Guyanese diaspora in UK; Commonwealth ties
  • Jamaica — CARICOM partner; comparable English-speaking Caribbean legal tradition
