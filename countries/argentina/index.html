Dual Citizenship

Argentina fully recognizes and permits dual (or multiple) nationality. The 1994 constitutional reform removed earlier restrictions, and Argentine law does not require renunciation of any prior citizenship upon naturalization. Argentine citizens who acquire a foreign nationality are equally protected — Argentina does not strip citizenship for naturalizing elsewhere.

This makes Argentina one of the most permissive dual-citizenship jurisdictions in Latin America. There are no bilateral treaties restricting this right, and no reciprocity conditions apply. The only practical nuance is that Argentine law requires nationals to enter and exit the country on their Argentine passport, but this creates no conflict with holding additional passports.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Argentina applies unrestricted jus soli. Every person born on Argentine soil is automatically an Argentine national at birth, regardless of the immigration status or nationality of the parents. This is one of the most expansive birthright citizenship rules in the world and is enshrined in Article 75 of the 1994 Constitution along with the Civil and Commercial Code.

There is no exception for children of diplomats in the constitutional text, though in practice the children of foreign diplomatic personnel are typically considered to acquire citizenship of the parents’ state rather than Argentina.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

ScenarioRule
Child born abroad, one Argentine parentArgentine nationality from birth
Child born abroad, both Argentine parentsArgentine nationality from birth
Generational limitNone — descent is unlimited
Registration requiredYes — at a civil registry or Argentine consulate

Argentine descent citizenship is unlimited in generations. A great-grandchild born in another country of an Argentine ancestor may claim Argentine nationality by descent, provided the chain of descent can be documented. Each generation must have registered or documented the connection.

Registration Process: Children born abroad must be inscribed in the Civil Registry of the Argentine Consulate in the country of birth or in the Civil Registry in Argentina. This generates the Argentine birth certificate. Adult descendants claiming through prior generations must compile documentary proof of each link in the chain.

Required documents for descent claim:

  • Birth certificate of the claimant
  • Birth certificate(s) proving the Argentine ancestor
  • Marriage certificates linking generations (where applicable)
  • All documents apostilled and translated into Spanish

By Adoption

Children adopted by Argentine nationals acquire Argentine nationality under the same rules as biological children. Full adoption integrates the child into the family for all legal purposes, including nationality. The adoption decree must be issued or recognized by Argentine courts. Minors adopted abroad by Argentine nationals should be registered at the nearest consulate.

By Marriage

Marriage to an Argentine national does not automatically confer citizenship, but it reduces the standard naturalization period.

CategoryResidency Required
Spouse of Argentine national2 years (standard; some judicial decisions allow reduction)

Note: There is no automatic citizenship upon marriage. The spouse must still meet residency requirements and apply through the naturalization process.

Argentina’s 2-year naturalization period is among the shortest in the world and is a major draw for those seeking a second passport. The process is judicial — the applicant petitions a federal judge rather than an administrative agency.

Reduced Residency Categories

The Ibero-American preference reflects Argentina’s broader regional integration commitments and applies to nationals of Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Uruguay, and Venezuela.

Preferred Pathways

Fastest route for most applicants: Obtain permanent residency → reside 2 years → file judicial naturalization petition. No language test, no civics exam, no integration course. This is one of the simplest pathways globally.

For Ibero-Americans and Spaniards: The 1-year reduced track makes Argentine citizenship achievable within 12–18 months of arrival.

MERCOSUR Residency Agreement: Nationals of MERCOSUR member and associate states (Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru) can access permanent residency relatively quickly under the Patria Grande program, which accelerates the path to the 2-year naturalization clock.

Argentine passport power: The Argentine passport provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 171 countries, including the Schengen Area, the United Kingdom, Japan, and most of the Americas. This ranks it among the stronger passports in Latin America.

Special Clauses & Programs

No Renunciation Required: Unlike several neighbors, Argentine law does not require applicants to formally renounce prior nationality.

Judicial Naturalization: Applications are processed by federal judges (Juzgados Federales). The judge may request an interview. Processing times vary by jurisdiction — Buenos Aires federal courts tend to have heavier caseloads.

Children of Naturalized Citizens: A child born after the parent naturalizes is Argentine by birth if born in Argentina (jus soli) or by descent if born abroad.

Recovery of Nationality for Exile Victims: Argentina has special provisions for individuals who lost or were unable to exercise nationality during the 1976–1983 military dictatorship. Descendants of exiles and forcibly displaced persons may have expedited processes at the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Argentina does not offer a formal citizenship-by-investment program. There is no direct route to citizenship through capital transfer alone.

Residency by Investment (RBI)

Argentina offers a rentista (passive income) residency category and an investment-based residency:

CategoryRequirementNotes
RentistaProof of regular income from abroad (pensions, dividends, etc.)Income must cover basic needs; no fixed minimum in USD
Investor (Inversionista)Capital investment in Argentina — historically interpreted as ARS 1.5M+ (subject to inflation adjustments)Requires business plan or proof of investment
Real estateNo dedicated visa; can support rentista applicationNo direct RBI via real estate

Path to citizenship: Any residency category that leads to permanent residency starts the 2-year naturalization clock. This makes even the modest income-based residency a viable route to citizenship within approximately 3–4 years total.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Argentina is South America’s third-largest economy (GDP ~$640 billion USD in 2024) with a highly educated workforce, strong agricultural and energy sectors, and a growing technology industry. Buenos Aires is a major regional startup hub. However, the country faces persistent macroeconomic challenges including high inflation, currency controls, and periodic debt restructuring.

The SAS (introduced by Law 27,349 in 2017) is the preferred vehicle for startups and entrepreneurs — it can be formed online within 24 hours.

Entrepreneur & Startup Visas

Argentina offers the “Visado Especial para Emprendedores” (Special Entrepreneur Visa) under Law 27,349 (Ley de Apoyo al Capital Emprendedor):

  • Designed for foreign entrepreneurs seeking to establish or scale startups in Argentina
  • Applicant must present a business plan and demonstrate the viability of the venture
  • Allows temporary residency with a path to permanent residency
  • Buenos Aires City also runs its own startup ecosystem programs (Buenos Aires Tech) with support for visa processes

Argentina has a complex and frequently changing tax environment. The country has introduced several “blanqueo” (tax amnesty) programs and currency externalization schemes. Foreign investors should note exchange controls (CEPO cambiario) which affect repatriation of profits, though these have fluctuated significantly with each administration.

The “dólar blue” informal exchange market has historically offered significantly different rates than the official rate. This creates real complexity for foreign businesses but also opportunities for those receiving USD income.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Zonas Francas (Free Zones): 24 free trade zones across the country offering customs and tax exemptions for manufacturing and logistics
  • Régimen de Promoción de la Economía del Conocimiento (Knowledge Economy Law): Tax benefits for software, IT, biotechnology, and creative industries — up to 60% reduction in income tax
  • Vaca Muerta: Major shale oil/gas investment incentive zone in Neuquén Province with special fiscal stability guarantees
  • RIGI (Régimen de Incentivo para Grandes Inversiones): Introduced 2024 — large-scale investment incentive offering 30-year fiscal stability for investments over $200M USD in energy, mining, infrastructure, and technology

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

Argentine nationals may voluntarily renounce citizenship by filing a formal declaration before a competent authority (civil registry or consulate abroad). Renunciation is irrevocable but the individual may subsequently re-acquire nationality through naturalization if they later reside in Argentina and meet requirements.

Involuntary Loss

CauseRule
Acquisition of foreign nationalityDoes NOT cause loss — dual nationality fully permitted
Serving in foreign armed forcesNot explicitly codified as automatic loss under current law
Conviction of certain crimesNot a ground for loss under current constitutional framework
Fraudulent acquisitionNaturalization may be revoked by judicial decree within a limited period

Note: Argentina’s 1994 Constitution significantly curtailed grounds for involuntary loss of nationality. The country moved strongly toward protecting nationality as a fundamental right.

Recovery / Resumption

Persons who lost Argentine nationality under prior regimes (pre-1994) or through voluntary renunciation may apply for re-naturalization under the standard process. There is no special “recovery” track per se, but courts have discretion to expedite for persons with strong prior connections to Argentina.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Valid passport (all pages, certified copy)
  • Argentine DNI (Documento Nacional de Identidad) — permanent residents only
  • Proof of 2 years continuous residence in Argentina (rental contracts, utility bills, tax filings, etc.)
  • Certificate of permanent residency (issued by Dirección Nacional de Migraciones)
  • Criminal background check from country of origin (apostilled, translated)
  • Argentine criminal background check (Certificado de Antecedentes Penales — ANSES/Policía Federal)
  • Birth certificate (apostilled, translated into Spanish)
  • Marriage certificate if applicable (apostilled, translated)
  • Two passport-size photos
  • Completed petition form (provided by the federal court)
  • Payment of court filing fee
