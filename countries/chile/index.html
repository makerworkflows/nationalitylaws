Dual Citizenship

Chile fully permits dual and multiple nationality. There is no provision in Chilean law requiring a national to renounce prior citizenship upon acquiring Chilean nationality, and there is no requirement to renounce Chilean citizenship when acquiring a foreign one. This policy was firmly established in the 1980 Constitution (as amended) and reinforced by the Ley de Migración e Extranjería 21.325 enacted in 2021.

Chilean nationals living abroad may acquire foreign citizenship without any risk to their Chilean status. Naturalized Chileans retain their prior nationality unless they voluntarily renounce it. Chile’s liberal dual citizenship stance has made it a preferred destination for long-term residency and naturalization in South America.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Chile applies jus soli with a limited exception. All persons born on Chilean territory are Chilean nationals at birth, except children born to:

  • Foreign parents who are both in Chile as tourists or transient visitors at the time of birth
  • Foreign diplomatic personnel accredited to Chile

This exception is narrower than it may seem in practice. Parents who hold any form of temporary or permanent residency — even early in their residency process — generally transmit birthright citizenship to their children. The exception primarily targets newborns of parents who have never sought residency.

The 2021 immigration reform (Ley 21.325) clarified and codified these rules.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

ScenarioRule
Child born abroad, one or both parents ChileanChilean nationality from birth
Generational limitFirst generation only for automatic transmission abroad
Registration requiredYes — at a Chilean consulate within 1 year of birth (recommended)
Adult claiming by descentMay apply through consulate; requires registration of birth

Children born abroad to at least one Chilean parent are Chilean nationals at birth by descent. However, the chain of automatic transmission is one generation — a child born abroad to a Chilean parent who was also born abroad does not automatically acquire citizenship and may need to establish a prior connection to Chile.

Registration Process: The birth must be registered at the Civil Registry (Registro Civil) in Chile or at a Chilean consulate. Late registration is possible but may require additional documentation.

Documents for descent claim:

  • Birth certificate of the claimant (apostilled)
  • Proof of Chilean parent’s nationality (Chilean identity card or passport)
  • Birth certificate of Chilean parent
  • Marriage certificate if applicable
  • All foreign documents apostilled and translated into Spanish

By Adoption

Minors adopted by Chilean nationals acquire Chilean nationality upon completion of the legal adoption process. The adoption decree from Chilean courts (or a recognized foreign adoption) grants full nationality rights equivalent to those of a biological child. Adopted children should be registered at the Civil Registry.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Chilean national does not automatically grant citizenship. However, spouses of Chilean nationals benefit from a reduced naturalization period and certain residency preferences.

The Ibero-American preference is one of the most generous in the region. It applies to nationals of: Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Uruguay, and Venezuela.

Preferred Pathways

Fastest route for Ibero-Americans: Obtain residency → reside 1 year → apply for naturalization. Total timeline approximately 18–24 months from arrival.

Standard route: Obtain temporary residency (up to 2 years) → convert to permanent residency → accumulate 5 years → apply for naturalization.

Ley 21.325 (2021 Immigration Reform): Chile’s modernized immigration law created new residency categories including:

  • Visa de Residencia Temporaria: Up to 2 years, renewable
  • Residencia Definitiva: Permanent residency
  • The new law simplified procedures, introduced digital processing, and improved protection for vulnerable migrants

Chilean passport strength: The Chilean passport grants visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 174 countries, the strongest in South America and among the top 20 globally. It provides access to the Schengen Area, United Kingdom, Japan, United States (ESTA), Canada (eTA), and most of the world’s major economies.

Special Clauses & Programs

Constitutional Review (2022–2025): Chile undertook two constitutional reform processes. The nationality provisions remained stable through both processes, preserving jus soli (with exceptions) and jus sanguinis.

Born in Chile Exception Resolution: Under the 2021 reform, children of undocumented parents born in Chile face more complex proceedings; those born before 2021 under older rules may have acquired nationality automatically.

Voluntary Return Programs: Chile has offered programs for Chileans abroad (including exiles from the Pinochet era 1973–1990) to facilitate return and reintegration, with support for nationality documentation.

No Language Exam: Although Spanish competency is assessed informally in interviews, there is no standardized language test for naturalization — making the process accessible to those with conversational ability.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Chile does not offer a citizenship-by-investment program. There is no direct route to citizenship through capital transfer.

Residency by Investment (RBI)

Chile does not have a formal golden visa or investor visa with a fixed investment threshold. However, investment and entrepreneurship pathways exist:

CategoryRequirementNotes
Investor VisaProof of capital investment or business activity in ChileNo fixed minimum; assessed case by case
Entrepreneur / Independent ProfessionalBusiness plan, proof of viabilityPopular for tech entrepreneurs
RentistaProof of regular income sufficient for living expensesPension income, dividends, etc.

Once any of these residency categories is obtained and maintained, the standard clock toward permanent residency and eventual naturalization begins.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Chile is Latin America’s most economically free and transparent country (Heritage Foundation Economic Freedom Score: 77.3, Global ranking ~23). Santiago is the region’s premier financial center and startup hub. The country benefits from strong institutions, rule of law, low corruption (CPI: 67 — highest in Latin America), political stability, and a business-friendly legal framework.

Chile is a member of the Pacific Alliance, OECD, and maintains an extensive network of free trade agreements covering 65+ countries including the US, EU, Japan, China, South Korea, and Australia.

Chile operates an integrated tax system where corporate taxes paid can be credited against personal income taxes owed on dividends, reducing effective double taxation. The country has one of the most comprehensive tax treaty networks in South America.

Chile has the most developed financial system in Latin America. The Comisión para el Mercado Financiero (CMF) provides strong regulatory oversight. The country’s fintech ecosystem is growing rapidly under the Ley Fintech (2023).

Key Programs & Incentives

  • InvestChile (CORFO): One-stop shop for foreign investors; facilitates market entry, partner matching, and incentives
  • Free Trade Zones: Iquique (ZOFRI) and Punta Arenas — duty-free trading zones for manufacturing and logistics
  • Franquicia SENCE: Training tax credit up to 1% of payroll for workforce development
  • R&D Tax Incentives: Up to 35% tax credit for certified R&D activities
  • Green Hydrogen Strategy: Major investment incentives for renewable energy and green hydrogen projects
  • Mining Investment: World’s largest copper producer; special fiscal regimes for large mining investments
  • Pacific Alliance: Preferential access to Colombia, Mexico, and Peru markets

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

Chilean nationals may renounce their citizenship voluntarily by submitting a formal declaration before the Civil Registry (Registro Civil) or a Chilean consulate abroad. Renunciation is recorded and the individual ceases to be Chilean. It is possible to re-acquire nationality through naturalization after establishing residency again.

Involuntary Loss

CauseRule
Acquisition of foreign nationalityDoes NOT cause loss — dual nationality fully permitted
Serving in a foreign military during war against ChileHistorical provision; rarely applied
Being a naturalized Chilean found to have misrepresented factsNaturalization may be revoked within 5 years
Conviction for terrorism or drug trafficking in certain contextsConstitutional provision (Article 17, No. 3 — serious crimes)

Article 17 of the Chilean Constitution lists grounds for loss of citizenship (ciudadanía, the political right to vote and hold office) which are distinct from nationality (nacionalidad). Loss of ciudadanía due to criminal conviction does not automatically mean loss of nationality.

Recovery / Resumption

Persons who lost Chilean nationality or citizenship may petition for its recovery through:

  • The Constitutional Court (Tribunal Constitucional) for cases involving constitutional articles
  • The Ministerio del Interior for administrative cases
  • Special legislation has been used historically to restore nationality to political exiles

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Valid passport (all pages, certified copy)
  • RUT (Rol Único Tributario) — Chilean tax ID
  • Cédula de Identidad Extranjería (foreigner ID card) or permanent residency certificate
  • Proof of continuous residency (rental contracts, utility bills, tax declarations, pension fund contributions)
  • Certificate of residency status from SERMIG
  • Criminal background check from country of origin (apostilled, translated)
  • Chilean background check (Certificado de Antecedentes — Registro Civil)
  • Birth certificate (apostilled, translated into Spanish)
  • Marriage certificate if applicable (apostilled, translated)
  • Two recent passport-size photos
  • Completed naturalization application form
  • Application fee payment receipt
