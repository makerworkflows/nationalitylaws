Dual Citizenship

Saint Lucia fully permits dual and multiple citizenship. A foreign national who acquires Saint Lucian citizenship through naturalization or investment is not required to renounce their existing nationality. Similarly, a Saint Lucian citizen who naturalizes abroad retains their Saint Lucian citizenship unless they formally renounce it. This open policy reflects Saint Lucia’s alignment with other Eastern Caribbean states on the issue of multiple nationalities and its interest in maintaining diaspora ties.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

A child born in Saint Lucia acquires citizenship at birth if at least one parent is a Saint Lucian citizen or lawful permanent resident at the time of birth. Birth on Saint Lucian soil to two foreign nationals temporarily present does not automatically confer citizenship.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Citizenship is transmitted to children born abroad when at least one parent holds Saint Lucian citizenship at the time of birth. Registration of the child’s citizenship is typically required. The right of transmission by descent applies to the first generation born abroad; subsequent generations may face additional requirements to establish or maintain citizenship.

By Adoption

A child lawfully adopted by a Saint Lucian citizen may be registered as a citizen of Saint Lucia. The adoption must comply with Saint Lucian law or be legally recognized in Saint Lucia. Adopted children are treated equivalently to biological children for citizenship registration purposes.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Saint Lucian citizen does not confer automatic citizenship. A foreign spouse may reside lawfully in Saint Lucia and apply for naturalization once the required residence period has been completed. There is no shortened pathway purely by virtue of marriage, though the spousal residency permit facilitates accumulation of qualifying residence time.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Foreign nationals may apply for Saint Lucian citizenship after 5 years of lawful ordinary residence. Requirements include:

  • 5 years continuous or near-continuous lawful residence
  • Good character — no disqualifying criminal record
  • English language ability (Saint Lucia is English-speaking; no standardized test administered)
  • Intention to remain resident, or at least maintain substantive ties to Saint Lucia
  • Oath of allegiance (Saint Lucia is a Commonwealth realm with King Charles III as head of state)
  • Payment of prescribed naturalization fees
  • Two character references from Saint Lucian citizens

Applications are submitted through the Department of Immigration and the Chief Immigration Officer. The Governor-General grants citizenship formally upon approval.

Citizenship / Residency by Investment

Saint Lucia Citizenship by Investment Program (CBI)

Saint Lucia established its Citizenship by Investment program in 2015 under the Saint Lucia Citizenship by Investment Act. It is administered by the Citizenship by Investment Unit (CBIU) under the Office of the Prime Minister. As one of the newer entrants among Caribbean CBI programs, Saint Lucia has distinguished itself through competitive pricing, flexible investment options — particularly the government bond route — and a straightforward process with no mandatory visit, no interview requirement, and no residency requirement.

No residency required. No visit required. No interview required.

Investment Options

Option 1: National Economic Fund (NEF) — Non-Refundable Contribution

The NEF is a direct non-refundable contribution to Saint Lucia’s national development fund. It is the simplest and most commonly chosen route.

Applicant ProfileRequired Contribution
Single applicantUSD $100,000
Applicant + up to 3 dependentsUSD $140,000
Each additional dependent (child)USD $25,000
Each additional qualifying parent/grandparentUSD $25,000

This is one of the most competitively priced NEF/NTF contributions in the Caribbean, undercutting Grenada ($150,000) and Antigua ($100,000 with lower dependent inclusion). For a single investor seeking the fastest, most direct route to citizenship, the Saint Lucia NEF at $100,000 is among the most accessible.

Option 2: Government Bonds — The Lowest Net-Cost Option

This is Saint Lucia’s most distinctive and strategically interesting CBI investment route. The investor purchases zero-interest, non-transferable government bonds that are held for a mandatory lock-up period and then returned in full.

Applicant ProfileBond Amount RequiredLock-Up Period
Single applicantUSD $250,0005 years
Applicant + spouseUSD $250,0005 years
Applicant + spouse + 2 dependentsUSD $250,0005 years

Key mechanics:

  • The bonds earn zero interest
  • After 5 years, the principal is returned in full to the investor
  • The bonds are non-transferable and non-pledgeable during the lock-up period
  • The true cost is the opportunity cost of USD $250,000 locked at 0% for 5 years, not the face value of the investment

Why this matters: For an investor who can park $250,000 for 5 years and values the time-value calculation, the actual net cost of citizenship may be lower than the NEF contribution. Using a conservative 4–5% annual opportunity cost on $250,000 over 5 years implies an “effective cost” of roughly $50,000–$62,500 in foregone returns — significantly less than the $100,000 NEF cash contribution. For investors with large liquid reserves who are not constrained by locking up $250,000, the bond route is often the rational economic choice.

Comparison to NEF: The NEF at $100,000 is gone permanently. The bond at $250,000 returns the full amount after 5 years. An investor with a 5-year horizon and available liquidity may mathematically prefer the bond.

Option 3: Approved Real Estate Investment

Investment in a government-approved real estate development project.

RequirementDetail

As with other Caribbean CBI real estate programs, the property must be in a pre-approved project. After 5 years, the property can be sold, and in some cases the new buyer may be eligible to use the same property for their own CBI application.

Option 4: Enterprise Investment

The highest-value option targeting major investors creating employment in Saint Lucia.

StructureMinimum Investment
Sole investorUSD $3,500,000
Joint venture (minimum 3 investors)USD $6,000,000 total (each investor minimum $1,000,000)

The enterprise must be in an approved sector and must create a minimum number of jobs for Saint Lucian nationals. This route is used primarily by institutional investors or developers bringing large projects to Saint Lucia and is not commonly used by individual citizenship-seeking investors.

Due Diligence Process

The Saint Lucia CBIU conducts a thorough multi-stage due diligence process:

  1. Authorized Agent Pre-Screening — All applications go through a licensed agent; no direct applications accepted
  2. CBIU Document Review — Verification of all submitted materials
  3. Independent Background Checks — Conducted by third-party international due diligence firms
  4. Security Vetting — Final clearance through government security bodies

Saint Lucia conducts due diligence on the main applicant and all adult dependents (18+).

Family Inclusion

Saint Lucia’s CBI program allows the following qualifying dependents:

  • Spouse (legally married)
  • Children under 26 who are financially dependent on the main applicant (including stepchildren and legally adopted children)
  • Parents and grandparents aged 65 and above who are financially dependent

Note: The age limit for dependent children (26) and the parental/grandparental age threshold (65) are somewhat stricter than Grenada’s program (30 and 55 respectively). Investors with older parents or adult children between 55–65 should compare programs carefully.

Passport Power

A Saint Lucian passport grants visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 145 destinations as of 2026, including:

  • Schengen Area (all 27 countries) — visa-free
  • United Kingdom — visa-free
  • Ireland — visa-free
  • Hong Kong — visa-free
  • Singapore — visa-free
  • Most of Latin America and the Caribbean — visa-free
  • United States — visa required
  • Canada — visa required
  • China — visa required (unlike Grenada, Saint Lucia does not have visa-free access to China)

Saint Lucia does not have a US E-2 Treaty Investor Visa arrangement, which is a notable disadvantage compared to Grenada for investors specifically seeking a pathway to US residency.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Company Formation

Saint Lucia offers a straightforward business registration environment for Caribbean standards:

  • International Business Company (IBC) — Available for offshore structures; exemption from local income tax on non-Saint Lucian-sourced income; Saint Lucia is an OECS member and has targeted offshore financial services development
  • Domestic Company (Limited by Shares) — Standard corporate structure for businesses operating locally
  • Limited Liability Partnership
  • Sole Proprietorship

The Companies Registry (under the Financial Services Regulatory Authority, FSRA) handles registration. Turnaround for a standard company registration is typically 3–7 business days with proper documentation.

Tax Overview

Saint Lucia has a moderate Caribbean tax regime:

  • Corporate income tax: 30%
  • Personal income tax: Progressive rates; top rate approximately 30% for residents
  • VAT: 12.5% standard rate
  • No capital gains tax
  • No inheritance tax
  • No wealth tax
  • Withholding tax on dividends: 15% (reduced by treaty in some cases)
  • IBCs: Tax exemptions on non-Saint Lucian-sourced income for qualifying international business structures

Saint Lucia is a member of the CARICOM tax information sharing framework and has signed various TIEAs. The FSRA regulates the financial services sector.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

Saint Lucia uses the Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD), pegged at XCD 2.70 per USD through the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB). Key banking institutions:

  • Bank of Saint Lucia — Largest domestic bank; government-affiliated
  • CIBC FirstCaribbean International Bank
  • Republic Bank (Saint Lucia) Ltd
  • Scotiabank (historically present; verify current operations)
  • Saint Lucia Development Bank — Development finance institution

The offshore financial center in Castries caters to international business, trusts, and captive insurance. CBI investors can typically open accounts at local banks, though non-resident account opening requires enhanced due diligence documentation.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Invest Saint Lucia: The national investment promotion agency; provides investor facilitation, permits support, and site selection assistance
  • Tourism incentives: Approved hotel and resort developments may qualify for import duty concessions and corporate tax holidays
  • Free zone concessions: Saint Lucia has explored free zone frameworks offering reduced taxation and customs benefits for qualifying export-oriented businesses
  • Fiscal Incentives Act: Provides tax holidays for qualifying pioneer industries and approved enterprises

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A Saint Lucian citizen of full age (18+) and capacity may renounce their citizenship by declaration before a designated officer. Renunciation takes effect upon registration. Renunciation may be withheld during wartime or a period of national emergency at ministerial discretion.

Involuntary Loss

Saint Lucian citizenship acquired by naturalization or CBI registration may be revoked if:

  • The citizenship was obtained through fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment of material facts
  • The holder is convicted of a serious criminal offense after grant of citizenship
  • The holder engages in activities seriously prejudicial to the vital interests of Saint Lucia
  • The holder voluntarily acquires and exercises the citizenship of another country in a manner inconsistent with their obligations (note: this is rarely applied given Saint Lucia’s permissive dual citizenship stance)

Revocation requires formal proceedings and is subject to ministerial approval and appeal rights. Citizenship by birth is not subject to deprivation.

Recovery / Resumption

Former Saint Lucian citizens who lost or voluntarily renounced citizenship may apply for re-registration or fresh naturalization. Recovery is not automatic and is reviewed on a case-by-case basis. Each application is considered on its individual merits.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For CBI applications (via authorized agent):

  • Valid passport (all pages) for all applicants and dependents
  • Birth certificates for all applicants and dependents
  • Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  • Divorce decree or death certificate of former spouse (if applicable)
  • Police clearance certificates from all countries of residence for the past 10 years (for all applicants 17+)
  • Medical certificate (health declaration; HIV test required)
  • Passport-size photographs (recent, meeting specification requirements)
  • Bank reference letter from a recognized financial institution
  • Proof of source of funds (bank statements 3–6 months, tax returns, business ownership documents)
  • Professional biography / curriculum vitae for main applicant
  • Completed CBIU application forms (obtained through authorized agent)
  • For NEF route: proof of contribution payment to government account
  • For bond route: bond subscription agreement and proof of payment
  • For real estate route: executed purchase agreement and proof of investment funds

For naturalization applications:

  • Identity documentation as above
  • Proof of 5 years lawful residence (immigration stamps, residence permits, entry/exit records)
  • Character references from Saint Lucian citizens
  • Clean criminal record from country of origin and any other country of significant residence

Where to Submit

  • CBI applications: Must be filed through a government-licensed Authorized Agent. The CBIU does not accept direct applications. Current list of authorized agents is available at cips.gov.lc.
  • Naturalization applications: Filed with the Department of Immigration and the Office of the Chief Immigration Officer in Castries, Saint Lucia.

Processing Time

  • CBI (NEF route): 3–4 months from complete application submission
  • CBI (Bond route): 3–4 months; bond subscription completed separately post-approval
  • CBI (Real estate route): 3–4 months after real estate investment documentation is complete
  • Naturalization: 6–12 months; varies based on case complexity and volume
