Dual Citizenship

Tunisia fully permits dual citizenship. Tunisian citizens who acquire a foreign nationality do not lose their Tunisian citizenship, and foreign nationals who naturalize in Tunisia are not required to renounce their existing nationality.

This has been the consistent position in Tunisian law for decades, making Tunisia one of the most liberal countries in the Arab world on the question of multiple nationality. The policy reflects several realities:

  1. Massive diaspora: Approximately 1.5 million Tunisians live abroad, representing roughly 12% of the population. The largest communities are in France (approximately 700,000), Italy (200,000+), Germany, Canada, and the Gulf States. These communities are economically vital — diaspora remittances consistently account for a significant share of Tunisia’s GDP.

  2. Historical ties to France: The colonial relationship with France (1881—1956) created deep cultural, linguistic, and family ties. Hundreds of thousands of Tunisians hold French citizenship alongside their Tunisian nationality. Any policy requiring renunciation would be politically and practically unworkable.

  3. Active government engagement: Tunisia maintains a dedicated portfolio for diaspora affairs — the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Migration and Tunisians Abroad — and the Office of Tunisians Abroad (OTE) provides consular, social, and cultural services to the diaspora.

Key implications for dual citizens:

  • Tunisian citizens who naturalize in France, Italy, Canada, or any other country retain their Tunisian nationality automatically, with no notification or retention application required.
  • They remain subject to Tunisian law when in Tunisia, including military service obligations for men (though enforcement for those permanently residing abroad is rare).
  • There are no restrictions on dual citizens holding public office, voting in Tunisian elections (including from abroad via consular voting), or owning property in Tunisia.
  • Dual citizens entering Tunisia must use their Tunisian passport (Tunisia requires its citizens to enter and exit on Tunisian documents).

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Sanguinis with Jus Soli Element)

Tunisian nationality is primarily acquired through descent (jus sanguinis). A child acquires Tunisian nationality at birth if:

  1. The father is Tunisian, regardless of place of birth (automatic, no declaration needed), OR
  2. The mother is Tunisian, regardless of place of birth (automatic since the 2010 amendment; previously required a declaration within one year of the child reaching majority).

The 2010 reform (Decree-Law No. 2010-55 of 1 December 2010) was a landmark change that equalized maternal and paternal transmission. Before 2010, a child born to a Tunisian mother and a non-Tunisian father could only acquire Tunisian nationality through a formal declaration process, creating a significant gender disparity. The 2010 amendment made maternal transmission automatic, aligning Tunisia with international human rights standards on gender equality in nationality law.

Conditional jus soli: A child born in Tunisia to a foreign father who was also born in Tunisia may claim Tunisian nationality by declaration within one year of reaching the age of majority (20 in Tunisia). This “double jus soli” provision is a feature inherited from French nationality law and applies to families with two generations born on Tunisian soil.

By Descent

Citizenship passes through both parents equally since the 2010 reform. Children born abroad to at least one Tunisian parent acquire nationality automatically, by operation of law. There is no generational limit, but documentary proof of the parent’s Tunisian citizenship is required.

Registration: Births abroad should be registered at the nearest Tunisian consulate. The consulate will issue a Tunisian birth certificate and enter the child in the national civil register. This registration is important for obtaining a Tunisian national identity card and passport.

By Adoption

Tunisian law, based on Islamic legal principles, does not recognize full adoption (adoption pleniere). The kafalah system (guardianship/foster care) is used instead. Kafalah does not create a legal parent-child relationship that automatically confers nationality. A child under kafalah retains their birth nationality.

However, a child under kafalah guardianship of a Tunisian citizen may apply for naturalization with the guardian’s support. The Ministry of Justice considers such applications favorably, particularly when the child has been raised in Tunisia from a young age.

By Marriage

A foreign woman married to a Tunisian man acquires the right to claim Tunisian nationality by declaration after at least 2 years of marriage, provided the couple has maintained continuous residence in Tunisia. The declaration is made to the Ministry of Justice.

Gender asymmetry: A foreign man married to a Tunisian woman does not have an equivalent right to acquire nationality by declaration. He must naturalize through the standard 5-year process. This gender disparity has been criticized by the CEDAW Committee and Tunisian women’s rights organizations. Despite the 2010 reform on maternal transmission to children, the marriage pathway remains unequal.

Naturalization is discretionary and granted by presidential decree on recommendation of the Ministry of Justice. Meeting all conditions does not guarantee approval. The process typically takes 12 to 24 months. Refusals are not required to be motivated (no reasons need to be given).

Special provisions: The President may naturalize persons who have rendered exceptional service to Tunisia without requiring the standard residency period.

Loss and Renunciation

Voluntary renunciation: A Tunisian citizen who holds another nationality may request release from Tunisian nationality (decheance or renonciation). The release must be authorized by presidential decree. Tunisian law is notably reluctant to grant renunciation — the government may refuse if it considers the person still has obligations to the state, such as:

  • Unfulfilled military service obligations.
  • Outstanding tax liabilities.
  • Pending legal proceedings in Tunisia.

This reluctance makes Tunisia one of the more difficult countries from which to formally renounce citizenship.

Involuntary loss (deprivation): Citizenship by naturalization may be withdrawn within 10 years of acquisition if:

  • Obtained by fraud, false representation, or concealment of material facts.
  • The person has been convicted of acts incompatible with Tunisian nationality.
  • The person has committed an offense against state security.

Citizens by birth are heavily protected against involuntary loss. Tunisia does not strip birth citizenship for acquiring a foreign nationality — consistent with its full acceptance of dual citizenship.

Restoration of Nationality

A person who lost Tunisian nationality by authorized release or by judicial decision may apply for reintegration (reintegration dans la nationalite tunisienne). The process requires a presidential decree and is discretionary.

Requirements for reintegration include:

  • Proof of prior Tunisian nationality.
  • Good character and no disqualifying criminal record.
  • Demonstration of continued connection to Tunisia (family ties, property, visits).
  • The process may take 12 to 24 months.

Stateless Persons and Foundlings

A child born in Tunisia to unknown parents is presumed to be Tunisian by operation of law. If parentage is later established and the child has a claim to another nationality, the presumption may be challenged, but only if the child would not be rendered stateless.

Tunisia is a party to the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and has constitutional provisions protecting the right to nationality. The 2022 Constitution affirms that every Tunisian citizen has the right to a nationality.

Document Checklist

  • Completed application form (obtained from Ministry of Justice or Tunisian consulate)
  • Birth certificate (extrait de naissance), original or certified copy, with apostille if foreign-issued
  • Valid passport or travel document
  • Tunisian national identity card (for descent claims) or parents’ identity documents
  • Marriage certificate (if applying through marriage)
  • Proof of 5 years habitual residence in Tunisia (for naturalization)
  • Police clearance certificate (bulletin No. 3 du casier judiciaire)
  • Medical certificate of good health
  • Proof of financial self-sufficiency (employment letter, bank statements, tax returns)
  • Passport-sized photographs (4)
  • Payment of prescribed fees (TND 200—500)
  • Military service certificate or exemption (for male applicants)
