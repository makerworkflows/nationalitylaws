Dual Citizenship

Peru formally recognized dual (and multiple) nationality through Law 26.574 (1996), passed in implementation of the 1993 Political Constitution. Prior to 1995, Peruvian nationality was lost automatically upon acquiring foreign citizenship. The 1995–1996 reform eliminated this rule entirely.

Today, Peruvian nationals who acquire a foreign citizenship do not lose their Peruvian nationality. Similarly, foreign nationals who naturalize in Peru are not required to formally renounce their prior citizenship under Peruvian law — though their prior country may have its own rules.

An important practical nuance: Peru’s naturalization statute technically states that applicants must renounce prior nationality as part of the oath. However, this renunciation is not enforced against the prior country, and Peru’s own Constitutional Tribunal has confirmed that Peru does not track or penalize dual nationality. Many applicants treat this as a formality while retaining their original citizenship.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Peru applies unrestricted jus soli. All persons born on Peruvian territory are Peruvian nationals at birth, regardless of the nationality or immigration status of the parents. Article 52 of the 1993 Constitution establishes this clearly.

There is no exception for children of tourists, transient visitors, or undocumented immigrants. The Registro Nacional de Identificación y Estado Civil (RENIEC) registers births and issues the Documento Nacional de Identidad (DNI) — the primary identity document.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

ScenarioRule
Child born abroad, one or both parents PeruvianPeruvian nationality from birth
Generational limitFirst generation — children of Peruvians born abroad
Registration requirementRegistration at RENIEC or Peruvian consulate
Adult claiming descentCan register at consulate if not previously registered

Children born abroad to at least one Peruvian national are Peruvian by descent at birth. Registration at the Peruvian consulate in the country of birth (or at RENIEC in Peru) is required to formally obtain a DNI and activate the nationality. Unregistered children may still claim nationality but will need to go through a regularization process.

Peruvian Diaspora Context: Peru has significant emigrant communities globally — approximately 3–4 million Peruvians live abroad, concentrated in the US, Chile, Argentina, Spain, Italy, Japan, and South Korea. The government has invested in consular services to facilitate nationality registration and documentation for these communities.

Documents for descent claim:

  • Birth certificate of the claimant (apostilled)
  • Proof of Peruvian parent’s nationality (DNI or Peruvian passport)
  • Birth certificate of Peruvian parent
  • Marriage certificate if applicable
  • All foreign documents apostilled and translated into Spanish

By Adoption

Children adopted by Peruvian nationals acquire Peruvian nationality upon the completion of the legal adoption process in Peru or through recognition of a foreign adoption decree by Peruvian courts. The adopted child is registered with RENIEC and issued a DNI. Peru is a signatory to the Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Peruvian national does not automatically confer Peruvian nationality. However, it provides certain residency and application preferences:

Peru’s 2-year naturalization period matches Argentina as among the shortest in South America and globally. The process is administered by the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores.

Preferred Pathways

Fastest standard route: Obtain resident visa → accumulate 2 years → apply for naturalization. This can be achieved in approximately 2.5–3 years from first arrival, accounting for processing times.

Descent route: Peruvians by descent who were never registered can claim nationality directly through RENIEC or consular registration without needing to establish residency — provided they can document the parental link.

Ibero-American advantage: Given Peru’s 2-year baseline, nationals of neighboring countries (Argentina, Chile, Bolivia, Brazil, Colombia, Ecuador) can achieve naturalization faster than in almost any other country in the region.

Peruvian passport access: Visa-free or visa-on-arrival to approximately 135 countries. Access includes the Schengen Area (with visa required for some), most of Latin America, select Asian countries. US, Canada, UK require visas.

Japan Connection: Peru has the second-largest Japanese diaspora in South America (Nikkei community, approximately 100,000–120,000 persons). Japanese-Peruvians may have pathways to both Peruvian nationality and consideration for Japanese ancestry-based long-term residency in Japan.

Special Clauses & Programs

Nikkei and Chinese-Peruvian Communities: Peru’s constitution and nationality law make no distinction between ethnic groups. Peruvians of Japanese (Nikkei) and Chinese (Tusán) descent are full citizens by birth or descent, and have historically held prominent positions in Peruvian society and government (including former Presidents Fujimori and Kuczynski).

Peruvian Law 30.878 (2019) — Simplification of Naturalization Procedures: Streamlined the administrative process, reduced documentation requirements, and introduced digital submission capabilities.

Ley de Retorno: Peru has offered periodic programs to facilitate the return of Peruvian emigrants, including customs facilitation for returning nationals.

Andean Community (CAN) Residency: Peru is a founding member of the Comunidad Andina (Andean Community) along with Bolivia, Colombia, and Ecuador. Decision 545 and 583 of CAN facilitate labor mobility and residency among member states, providing an accelerated path to residency for CAN nationals.

No Language Test: Although Spanish proficiency is expected and assessed informally in the interview, there is no standardized language examination — an advantage for immigrants from non-Spanish-speaking countries.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Peru does not offer a formal citizenship-by-investment program.

Residency by Investment (RBI)

Peru offers investment-based residency through the Visado de Negocios e Inversión framework:

CategoryRequirementNotes
Inversionista VisaMinimum investment of $30,000 USD in a Peruvian enterpriseRenewable; path to permanent residency
Rentista VisaMinimum $1,000 USD/month in guaranteed foreign incomePension, dividends, rental income
Empresa UnipersonalEstablish a single-owner businessLower barrier; needs viability demonstration
Real Estate InvestmentPurchase of property combined with income demonstrationNo dedicated “golden visa” per se

Once any residency category is obtained and maintained continuously, the 2-year naturalization clock applies. This makes Peru one of the most accessible investment-to-citizenship pathways in the region.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Peru is South America’s sixth-largest economy (GDP ~$263 billion USD) and one of the fastest-growing in the region over the past two decades, driven by mining exports (copper, gold, silver — Peru is a top-5 global producer), agriculture, and a growing services sector. Lima is a regional business hub with a vibrant startup ecosystem, strong consumer market, and increasing international investment.

Peru has maintained macroeconomic stability (low inflation, controlled public debt) while navigating significant political instability at the executive level. The business environment is generally open, with strong property rights, a competitive tax regime, and growing digital infrastructure.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Special Economic Zones: Paita (Piura), Ilo (Moquegua), Matarani (Arequipa), and Tacna free zones offering customs and tax exemptions
  • Mining Investment Regimes: Stability agreements (convenios de estabilidad) for large mining investments guaranteeing fiscal conditions for 10–15 years
  • ProInversión: Peru’s private investment promotion agency; facilitates concessions, public-private partnerships, and foreign direct investment
  • Andean Community Preferences: Preferential trade access with Bolivia, Colombia, and Ecuador
  • Tourism Investment: Special incentives for hotels and tourism infrastructure (Ley 29408)
  • Agriculture Export Promotion (Ley Agraria): Favorable labor and tax conditions for agro-export companies (under revision as of 2025)

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

Peruvian nationals may renounce their nationality voluntarily by appearing before RENIEC (inside Peru) or a Peruvian consulate (abroad) and executing a formal renunciation declaration. The renunciation is irrevocable unless the individual subsequently re-establishes residence and applies for reinstatement.

Involuntary Loss

CauseRule
Acquisition of foreign nationalityDoes NOT cause loss — Law 26,574 guarantees this
Entering service of a foreign state in conflict with PeruHistorically possible; not actively enforced
Fraudulent naturalizationNaturalization can be revoked by executive resolution
Renunciation oath in foreign naturalizationPeru does not act on foreign renunciation oaths

Peru’s post-1995 framework strongly protects nationality as a right. Involuntary loss is limited to fraudulent acquisition cases.

Recovery / Resumption

Peruvians who voluntarily renounced nationality can recover it through:

  1. Re-establishing legal residence in Peru
  2. Filing a recovery petition with the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores
  3. For those who lost nationality under pre-1995 rules upon acquiring foreign citizenship: recovery is available through a simplified administrative petition — this has been used by thousands of Peruvians abroad who lost nationality before the 1995 reform

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Valid passport (certified copy of all pages)
  • Carnet de Extranjería (foreigner ID card) or current residency permit
  • Proof of 2 years continuous legal residence (entry/exit records, tax filings, rental contracts, utility bills)
  • Criminal background check from country of origin (apostilled, Spanish translation)
  • Peruvian criminal background certificate (RENIEC or Policía Nacional del Perú)
  • Birth certificate (apostilled, Spanish translation)
  • Marriage certificate if applicable (apostilled, translated)
  • Recent passport-size photos (2–4 photos)
  • Certificate of legal residence from Superintendencia Nacional de Migraciones
  • Proof of economic solvency (bank statements, employment contract, or business registration)
  • Completed application form (Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores)
  • Application fee payment receipt
