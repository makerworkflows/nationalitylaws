Dual Citizenship

Yes — Malta allows dual and multiple citizenship with no restrictions. Maltese citizens are not required to renounce any existing nationality when acquiring Maltese citizenship, and acquiring a foreign nationality does not cause loss of Maltese citizenship. This applies equally to citizenship acquired through the MEIN investment program, naturalization, descent, or marriage.

Critically: Maltese citizenship is European Union citizenship. Holders have the full right to live, work, study, and retire in any of the 27 EU member states, plus access to EU travel documents and EU diplomatic protection abroad.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Malta does not grant citizenship based purely on birth on Maltese territory. A child born in Malta to foreign nationals does not automatically acquire Maltese citizenship by place of birth alone. Children born in Malta to at least one Maltese parent acquire citizenship at birth.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Maltese citizenship is transmitted by descent through either parent regardless of the country of birth, provided the Maltese parent was themselves a citizen at the time of the child’s birth. There is no generational cap — Maltese citizenship can be passed down indefinitely through the bloodline, though each generation must register the claim.

Citizenship by Ancestry (Act of 2020): Malta enacted significant amendments in 2020 (Act XXXI of 2020) specifically to allow descendants of Maltese emigrants to reclaim citizenship. Key provisions:

  • Descendants of Maltese nationals who emigrated and may have lost citizenship under earlier restrictive laws can now register
  • Applies to children, grandchildren, and in some cases great-grandchildren of Maltese emigrants
  • Must prove the Maltese lineage through certified documentation
  • No residency requirement for registration by descent

Prior restrictions corrected: Before 2000, Malta had gender-discriminatory rules (citizenship passed only through the father). Amendments in 2000 corrected this and allowed retroactive citizenship through the maternal line.

By Adoption

Children adopted by Maltese citizens acquire Maltese citizenship upon completion of the legal adoption process, with the same rights as biological children. The adoption must be recognized under Maltese law.

By Marriage

Foreign nationals married to Maltese citizens may apply for citizenship by registration after 5 years of marriage, provided they have been ordinarily resident in Malta for at least 5 years at the time of application. The marriage must be subsisting.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Standard requirements:

  • 5 years of lawful ordinary residence in Malta immediately preceding the application
  • Good character (no serious criminal convictions)
  • Adequate knowledge of Maltese or English (no formal language test — assessed at interview)
  • Intention to continue residing in Malta or to remain connected to Malta

Alternative: 1-year residency for:

  • Persons who have served in the Armed Forces of Malta
  • Persons with distinguished service to Malta

There is no formal language examination — the assessment is conducted as part of the interview with Identity Malta.

Special Clauses & Programs

EU Citizenship via Investment

Malta is one of only two EU member states (alongside Cyprus, which suspended its program in 2020) to have offered direct citizenship by investment. This makes Malta’s MEIN program uniquely valuable — no other EU CBI program is currently active.

ECJ Legal Challenge: In 2022, the European Commission referred Malta to the European Court of Justice (ECJ), arguing that selling citizenship violates the principle that EU citizenship should reflect a “genuine link” to a member state. As of early 2026, Malta has continued defending the program, arguing the genuine residency and due diligence requirements create an authentic connection. The case remains pending — applicants should monitor developments, as an adverse ECJ ruling could affect the program’s future status.

Mediterranean Climate & Quality of Life

Malta consistently ranks among the top countries for quality of life for expats, with a warm Mediterranean climate, English as an official language, strong healthcare (ranked 5th globally by WHO), and a low crime rate.

Investment Pathways

Due Diligence

MEIN employs a rigorous 4-tier due diligence process — one of the strictest in the world:

  1. Initial checks by the licensed registered mandatory (authorized agent)
  2. Community Malta Agency internal review
  3. Independent external due diligence provider
  4. Security and intelligence vetting

Source of funds documentation, clean criminal record (all countries of residence for 10+ years), and no adverse findings from international watchlists are mandatory.

Physical Presence

Applicants must establish genuine residence in Malta during the residency period:

  • 1-year track: physically present in Malta for 12 months before citizenship is granted
  • 3-year track: physically present for 36 months
  • A Maltese residence card must be obtained and maintained
  • Physical presence is verified — this is not a nominal requirement

Family Inclusion

The MEIN program allows a principal applicant to include dependents in a single application. Additional contributions are required for each dependent:

All dependents undergo the same due diligence process as the principal applicant.

What You Gain

  • Maltese passport — Henley Passport Index rank ~6, visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to ~188 countries
  • EU citizenship — right of free movement and residence in all 27 EU member states
  • Schengen access — visa-free travel across the Schengen Area
  • No wealth tax, no inheritance tax
  • Access to EU healthcare systems, EU education, EU social benefits
  • Children born after naturalization are Maltese citizens from birth

Historical Context: MIIP

The original Malta Individual Investor Programme (MIIP) launched in 2014 and was suspended in 2020 after the EU Commission began proceedings. The MEIN replaced it with stricter residency requirements (the MIIP had no genuine residency requirement — a key point of EU criticism). The MEIN’s mandatory 12 or 36-month physical residency was designed to address the “genuine link” argument.

Tax Overview

Malta’s tax system is one of the most favorable in the EU due to its full imputation and tax refund system:

Inheritance taxNone

How the refund system works: A Maltese company pays 35% corporate tax. When dividends are distributed to shareholders, they can claim a refund of 6/7ths of the tax paid (on trading income), resulting in an effective tax rate of approximately 5%. This is entirely legal and EU-compliant, though under pressure from EU minimum tax directives.

Global Minimum Tax: Malta is implementing the OECD/EU Global Minimum Tax (Pillar Two, 15% minimum) for large multinational groups (€750M+ revenue). Smaller companies retain the existing refund mechanism.

For MEIN holders: Malta taxes residents on remittance basis for foreign-source income (income remitted to Malta is taxed; income kept abroad is generally not). No wealth tax, no inheritance tax.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

  • Strong banking sector: Bank of Valletta, HSBC Malta, APS Bank, Lombard Bank
  • Malta is an EU member — SEPA transfers, Euro currency, EU financial regulation
  • Fintech and gaming companies widely served by specialized banking providers
  • Cryptocurrency regulation: Malta was among the first EU states to pass comprehensive crypto legislation (Virtual Financial Assets Act)
  • Remote opening limited — most banks require in-person visit for account setup

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Malta Enterprise — government agency for investment promotion; grants, tax credits for R&D and investment
  • MDIA (Malta Digital Innovation Authority) — regulator for blockchain, DLT, and AI; innovative sandbox
  • Gaming Authority (MGA) — leading EU gaming license jurisdiction (many online gaming companies HQ in Malta)
  • MFSA (Malta Financial Services Authority) — banking, insurance, investment funds regulation
  • EU Structural Funds — Malta receives EU cohesion funding for infrastructure and innovation
  • Highly Qualified Persons Rules — flat 15% income tax rate for qualifying professionals in regulated industries (financial services, gaming, aviation)

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A Maltese citizen who has reached adulthood and holds another citizenship may renounce Maltese citizenship by declaration. Renunciation is not permitted if it would render the person stateless. Renunciation takes effect upon registration.

Involuntary Loss

Maltese citizenship may be involuntarily lost in limited circumstances:

  • Serving in the armed forces of a foreign state without government authorization
  • Taking an oath of allegiance to a foreign state incompatible with Maltese citizenship (assessed case by case)
  • Citizenship obtained by fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment of material fact may be revoked

Deprivation of citizenship cannot result in statelessness.

Recovery / Resumption

Former Maltese citizens who lost citizenship involuntarily (e.g., under the pre-2000 gender-discriminatory rules, or through marriage under old laws) may apply for re-registration. Descendants affected by historical loss can invoke the 2020 amendments for ancestry-based registration.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For MEIN (CBI) applications:

  • Completed MEIN application form
  • Valid passport (all pages)
  • Birth certificate (apostilled)
  • Police clearance certificates from all countries of residence (past 10 years)
  • Proof of health insurance (covering Malta and EU)
  • Source of funds documentation (bank statements, investment records, audited accounts, proof of business income)
  • Proof of residential address in Malta (rental agreement or property purchase documentation)
  • Marriage/divorce certificates (if applicable)
  • Dependents’ birth certificates and passports
  • Proof of NGO donation (€10,000)
  • Real estate documentation (lease or title deed)

For naturalization (standard):

  • Birth certificate (apostilled and translated)
  • Valid passport
  • Criminal record certificate
  • Proof of 5 years lawful residence in Malta
  • Employment/financial records

Where to Submit

MEIN applications: Must be submitted through a Licensed Registered Mandatory (authorized agent) — direct submission is not permitted. The Community Malta Agency maintains a public register of licensed agents.

Standard naturalization: Community Malta Agency (Identity Malta), 3 Triq Evans, Blata l-Bajda, HMR 1514, Malta.

From abroad for ancestry/descent registration: Nearest Maltese embassy or consulate.
