Dominica

Last Updated: 2026-03-30

Official Portal: https://www.dominica.gov.dm

Embassy Finder: /embassies/dm

Important: This page covers the Commonwealth of Dominica — a sovereign island nation in the Eastern Caribbean. This is a completely separate country from the Dominican Republic, which is located on the island of Hispaniola and is a Spanish-speaking nation in the Greater Antilles. Dominica (ISO: DM) and the Dominican Republic (ISO: DO) are not the same place.

Dual Citizenship

The Commonwealth of Dominica fully permits dual and multiple citizenship. There is no requirement to renounce a prior nationality upon naturalization through residency or through the Economic Citizenship Program (ECP). Dominican citizens who acquire foreign nationality abroad are also free to retain their Dominican passport. This liberal dual citizenship policy is one of the factors that makes Dominica’s CBI program attractive — investors can add Dominican citizenship to their portfolio without sacrificing their existing nationality.

The Dominican passport provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 145 destinations worldwide, including all Schengen Area member states, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Singapore, Hong Kong, and most of the Caribbean. It does not provide visa-free access to the United States, Canada, or Australia. For investors from countries in South Asia, the Middle East, Africa, or Eastern Europe, the Dominican passport represents a major upgrade in travel freedom — particularly the Schengen and UK access, which would otherwise require lengthy visa application processes.

Dominica is a member of the Commonwealth of Nations, CARICOM, and the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS). CARICOM membership confers the right to seek employment in most other CARICOM member states without a work permit, providing regional mobility benefits beyond simple visa-free travel.

The Economic Citizenship Program, established in 1993, is the oldest operational citizenship by investment program in the Caribbean and one of the oldest in the world. It predates Antigua’s program by 20 years and has been refined over three decades of operation. Dominica is consistently cited as the best value citizenship by investment program globally at the USD $100,000 price point, earning this distinction from major immigration advisory firms, the Financial Times, and numerous independent analyses.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

A person born in Dominica is a citizen at birth if at least one parent is a Dominican citizen or is lawfully and ordinarily resident in Dominica at the time of birth. Children born in Dominica to two non-resident foreign nationals do not automatically acquire citizenship. The law follows a modified jus soli principle tied to parental status rather than birth location alone.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Dominican citizenship may be transmitted by descent to children born outside Dominica to a Dominican citizen parent. Registration must be completed within specified timeframes depending on generation. The first generation born abroad to a Dominican citizen parent has a relatively straightforward registration process through the nearest Dominican consulate or the Ministry of Justice. Second-generation and beyond claims become progressively more complex and may require registration within defined time windows.

By Adoption

A child lawfully adopted by a Dominican citizen may be registered as a Dominican citizen. The adoption must be recognized under Dominican law. The application is filed with the Ministry of Justice. Processing time varies depending on the complexity of the adoption and the jurisdictions involved.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Dominican citizen does not automatically confer citizenship. A foreign spouse may apply for registration as a citizen after a defined period of lawful residence as the spouse of a Dominican citizen. The standard naturalization residency requirement may be reduced for spousal applicants. The marriage must be genuine, and good character is required.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Standard naturalization requires five years of lawful ordinary residence in Dominica. Requirements:

  • Five years of continuous lawful residence (with permitted absences)
  • Good character (police clearances from Dominica and countries of prior residence)
  • Adequate knowledge of English or the ability to communicate in English
  • Oath of allegiance to the Commonwealth of Dominica
  • Demonstrated intention to continue to reside in Dominica

No formal language test or civics exam is mandated, though applicants may be interviewed. The application is filed with the Ministry of Justice. Processing for standard naturalization typically takes 12 to 18 months.

Citizenship / Residency by Investment

Overview

The Economic Citizenship Program (ECP) of the Commonwealth of Dominica was established by the Commonwealth of Dominica Citizenship by Investment Act in 1993, making it one of the founding citizenship-by-investment programs in the world. It is administered by the Citizenship by Investment Unit (CBIU) under the Office of the Prime Minister and the Ministry of Citizenship by Investment. The program operates exclusively through a network of licensed authorized agents and has been refined significantly over its 30+ year history.

Dominica’s ECP is widely ranked as the best value CBI program in the world for the following reasons:

  • Lowest price point for a single applicant in the Caribbean (USD $100,000 NDF contribution)
  • No residency requirement
  • No interview
  • No language test
  • No obligation to visit Dominica
  • Full family inclusion with broad dependent definitions
  • Strong due diligence and international reputation
  • Schengen + UK visa-free passport
  • Strong privacy framework for applicants

Investment Options

Option 1: Economic Diversification Fund (EDF) — Direct Contribution

The EDF contribution is a non-refundable donation to the Government of Dominica’s Economic Diversification Fund. The fund finances public infrastructure, healthcare, education, and environmental resilience projects — the last of which is significant given Dominica’s vulnerability to hurricanes (the island was devastated by Hurricane Maria in 2017 and is engaged in a major climate resilience reconstruction program).

At USD $100,000 for a single applicant, the EDF route is the most affordable pathway to a Schengen-capable, Commonwealth passport through citizenship by investment anywhere in the world.

Government processing and due diligence fees are charged separately (see Fees section below).

Approved real estate projects are listed by the CBIU and include resort and eco-resort developments, hotel shares, and boutique residential projects. Dominica’s real estate market is smaller and less developed than Antigua’s or St. Kitts’, but approved projects include internationally branded properties.

The 3-year holding period for Dominica real estate is shorter than Antigua’s 5-year requirement, which is an advantage for investors who prefer a faster exit strategy while still enjoying citizenship benefits.

Dominica has one of the most inclusive dependent definitions in the Caribbean, particularly regarding children up to age 30 (compared to age 26 for Antigua) and the inclusion of siblings of the main applicant. This makes Dominica particularly attractive for multi-generational family applications.

No Visit Requirement

Unlike Antigua and Barbuda, Dominica has no visit requirement for CBI citizens. There is no obligation to travel to Dominica before, during, or after the citizenship application process, and citizenship cannot be revoked for failure to visit. This makes Dominica particularly convenient for investors who view the passport purely as a travel and planning tool rather than a second home destination.

Due Diligence Process

The Dominica CBIU operates a four-stage due diligence framework:

  1. Authorized agent pre-screening: Licensed agents conduct initial client screening, AML/KYC checks, and document verification before submission
  2. CBIU internal review: The Unit reviews all documents and cross-checks against international watchlists and databases
  3. Independent third-party vetting: A globally recognized due diligence firm conducts background investigations, adverse media checks, and source-of-funds verification
  4. Government security clearance: Final sign-off from Dominican security and intelligence authorities

All adult dependents (16+) are subject to thorough due diligence. Dominica has blacklisted nationals of certain countries (primarily sanctioned states) from applying. The program has maintained a strong reputation among the Caribbean CBI programs for the rigor of its vetting — a factor that contributes to the passport’s continued acceptance in Schengen countries and the UK.

While Dominica’s passport reaches approximately 145 destinations versus Antigua’s ~152, the price differential for the EDF route is USD $25,000–$75,000 less expensive depending on family size, making it the preferred choice for purely cost-conscious investors.

Privacy and Data Protection

Dominica maintains strong privacy laws regarding citizenship records. The CBIU does not publicly disclose approved applicants’ names, nationalities of origin, or investment routes. This discretion is valued by investors from politically sensitive environments. However, Dominica does comply with international information exchange obligations under FATCA, CRS/AEOI, and treaties with bilateral partners.

Recent Developments

  • Dominica has faced increased scrutiny from the European Union regarding Caribbean CBI programs broadly, particularly following the EU’s consideration of suspending visa-free access to Schengen for CBI passport holders. As of 2025–2026, Dominica retains full Schengen visa-free access
  • The US has not imposed visa restrictions on Dominica CBI passports as of the last update
  • In 2023–2024, Dominica strengthened its authorized agent vetting requirements and suspended several agents for compliance failures
  • The government announced updates to the EDF contribution amounts in 2024; investors should verify current figures with a licensed agent at time of application
  • Hurricane Maria (2017) reconstruction continues to be a major national priority; a significant portion of EDF contributions fund climate resilience infrastructure

Business & Entrepreneurship

Company Formation

Dominica’s corporate legal framework is smaller and less developed than jurisdictions like Antigua or The Bahamas, but offers standard international business company (IBC) structures. The primary vehicle for international investors is the IBC:

  • Incorporated under the International Business Companies Act
  • Exempt from Dominica taxes on foreign-source income
  • Requires registered agent and registered address in Dominica
  • Directors and shareholders may be non-resident
  • No public filing of accounts required

Domestic companies operating within Dominica are subject to local licensing, tax, and regulatory requirements.

Dominica is not a pure zero-tax jurisdiction for residents conducting domestic business. However, for CBI holders who do not reside in Dominica, there are no tax obligations to Dominica. IBC structures holding foreign assets are tax-exempt on foreign income.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

Dominica uses the Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD), pegged at XCD 2.70 = USD 1.00, within the ECCB monetary union. Domestic banking is limited compared to larger Caribbean financial centers — this is not a hub for private banking or offshore wealth management at the scale of The Bahamas or Cayman Islands. The main banking institutions are regional Caribbean banks and the National Bank of Dominica.

CBI investors who do not reside in Dominica typically bank in their home country or in more developed offshore centers. Dominica citizenship does not in itself provide any particular banking advantage in third countries.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Fiscal Incentives Act: Tax holidays and duty exemptions for approved investment projects (manufacturing, tourism, agriculture)
  • Hotel Aid Act: Duty-free importation for hotel construction
  • Dominica Geothermal Energy Project: Dominica is developing geothermal energy capacity, with the ambition of becoming the first carbon-neutral nation; energy projects offer investment opportunities
  • Eco-tourism and agriculture: The government actively promotes investment in eco-tourism and sustainable agriculture given the island’s extraordinary natural environment (Dominica is nicknamed “The Nature Isle of the Caribbean”)

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A Dominican citizen of full age may renounce citizenship by making a formal declaration to the Minister responsible for citizenship. Renunciation may be withheld during periods of armed conflict or if deemed contrary to national interest. Once registered, renunciation is effective and the person ceases to be a Dominican citizen.

Involuntary Loss

CBI citizenship may be revoked if it was obtained through fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment of material facts during the application process. Citizenship may also be revoked from naturalized citizens for treasonous conduct or for serving in the armed forces of a state at war with Dominica. Citizenship by birth is substantially more difficult to revoke.

Unlike Antigua, Dominica does not have a visit requirement for CBI citizens, so there is no specific revocation ground tied to non-visitation.

Recovery / Resumption

A former Dominican citizen who lost citizenship through renunciation may apply for resumption. Resumption is subject to ministerial discretion and is not guaranteed. Those who had CBI citizenship revoked for fraud are generally ineligible for resumption.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For the ECP (CBI program):

  • Completed application forms (provided by licensed authorized agent)
  • Valid passport (all pages, current and all expired passports from past 10 years)
  • Certified birth certificate (with apostille where required)
  • Marriage/divorce certificates as applicable
  • Police clearances from all countries of residence in the past 10 years
  • Bank reference letter and source of funds/wealth documentation
  • Certified net worth statement or audited financials (for business applicants)
  • Physician’s medical certificate
  • Passport-size photographs (biometric standard; typically 6–8 per applicant)
  • Proof of investment (EDF contribution receipt, real estate purchase agreement, etc.)
  • CV / curriculum vitae

Where to Submit

All applications must be submitted through a licensed authorized agent registered with the CBIU. The CBIU does not accept direct applications.

Citizenship by Investment Unit Office of the Prime Minister Government of the Commonwealth of Dominica Roseau, Dominica

CBIU website: https://cbiu.gov.dm

Processing Time

  • Standard processing: 2–3 months from submission of a complete application
  • Dominica is consistently the fastest major Caribbean CBI jurisdiction for processing time
  • No physical presence in Dominica is required at any stage of the process
  • Passports may be collected in Dominica or through diplomatic missions in some cases

Fees

See Investment Options section for full fee schedule. For a single applicant via EDF: budget approximately USD $118,000–$125,000 total (contribution + government fees + agent fee). For a family of four: approximately USD $218,000–$225,000 total.

Related Countries

  • Antigua and Barbuda — Second most affordable Caribbean CBI; Schengen + UK access
  • St. Kitts and Nevis — Oldest CBI program in the world (1984); higher price point
  • Grenada — Caribbean CBI + E-2 US treaty investor visa eligibility
  • St. Lucia — Caribbean CBI with bond and real estate options
  • The Bahamas — No CBI; strong passport; zero income tax; 10-year residency naturalization
