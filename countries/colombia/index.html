Dual Citizenship

Colombia has permitted dual (and multiple) nationality without restriction since the 1991 Political Constitution, which eliminated the prior requirement to renounce foreign citizenship upon naturalization. Article 96 of the 1991 Constitution establishes that Colombian nationality is not lost when another nationality is acquired.

This means:

  • Colombian nationals who acquire foreign citizenship remain Colombian
  • Foreign nationals who naturalize as Colombian are not required to renounce their prior nationality
  • Colombia is one of the few Latin American countries with this policy codified explicitly at the constitutional level

This makes Colombia one of the most attractive naturalization destinations in the region — the holder retains all prior nationality rights while gaining a Latin American passport with strong regional visa-free access.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Colombia applies unrestricted jus soli. All persons born on Colombian territory are Colombian nationals at birth, regardless of the immigration or nationality status of the parents. This includes children born to undocumented immigrants — there is no exception for children of tourists or transient visitors in the constitutional text.

Article 96(a) of the 1991 Constitution explicitly establishes: “Los naturales de Colombia” — those born in Colombia — are Colombian nationals. The Colombian Constitutional Court has consistently upheld this broad interpretation.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

ScenarioRule
Child born abroad, father or mother Colombian nationalColombian nationality from birth
Generational limitFirst generation (children of Colombians born abroad)
Domicile requirement for descentChild must establish domicile in Colombia or register at consulate
RegistrationRequired at Registro Civil or Colombian consulate

Under Article 96(b) of the 1991 Constitution, children born abroad to at least one Colombian parent acquire Colombian nationality from birth, provided they:

  1. Are registered at a Colombian consulate abroad, OR
  2. Establish domicile (residencia) in Colombia

The domicile requirement creates a practical condition: a child born abroad to Colombian parents who does not register at a consulate must physically relocate to Colombia and establish domicile to activate the nationality claim. Consulate registration is the standard route for expatriate families.

By Adoption

Colombian law recognizes two forms of adoption-based nationality:

  1. Adoption by Colombian nationals: A minor adopted by Colombian nationals abroad acquires Colombian nationality upon completion of the adoption process and registration of the adoption at a Colombian consulate or the Registro Civil.
  2. Adoption of Colombian nationals by foreigners: The child retains Colombian nationality unless voluntarily renounced upon reaching majority.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Colombian national provides a reduced naturalization track but does not automatically confer citizenship.

The 1-year Ibero-American track is one of the most used pathways. Nationals of: Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Chile, Costa Rica, Cuba, Dominican Republic, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Paraguay, Peru, Portugal, Spain, Uruguay, and Venezuela all qualify.

Preferred Pathways

Digital Nomad / Remote Worker Route: Colombia introduced the Visa de Nómada Digital in 2022 — one of the most competitive digital nomad visas in Latin America. Remote workers earning $700+ USD/month can live in Colombia legally, with a path to longer-term residency.

Fastest route for Ibero-Americans: Arrive → obtain migrant visa → 1 year → apply for naturalization. Total timeline approximately 18–24 months.

Investment Route: Obtain M-10 Investor Visa → establish residency → path to naturalization within 5 years standard, potentially 1 year if Ibero-American.

Colombian passport access: Visa-free or visa-on-arrival to approximately 150 countries including Schengen Area (since 2015), most of Latin America, and key emerging markets. US and Canada still require visas.

Special Clauses & Programs

No Renunciation — Constitutional Guarantee: Colombia’s dual nationality guarantee is at the constitutional level, making it extremely stable and difficult to reverse through ordinary legislation.

Ley de Retorno Colombiano: Programs supporting Colombian nationals abroad who wish to return, including customs facilitation for household goods and vehicle importation.

Carta de Naturaleza vs. Resolución de Naturalización: Colombia has two naturalization instruments:

  1. Carta de Naturaleza: Granted by the President of the Republic for exceptional merit or services to Colombia. Highest form of naturalization.
  2. Resolución de Naturalización: Standard administrative route through the Cancillería for residency-based applicants.

Venezolanos con Estatus de Protección Temporal (ETPV): Since 2021, Colombia has offered Temporary Protection Status to Venezuelan migrants — approximately 2.5 million Venezuelans in Colombia — with a 10-year protected status and a pathway toward residency and eventual naturalization. This is the largest humanitarian regularization program in Latin American history.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Colombia does not offer a formal citizenship-by-investment program.

Residency by Investment (RBI)

Colombia offers investment-based visas through the Migrant Visa (M) and Resident Visa (R) system (reformed by Resolution 5477 of 2022):

Visa CategoryInvestment ThresholdNotes
M-10 — Business InvestorMin. 100 SMLMV (~$28,000 USD) investment in Colombian company or real estateRenewable; path to resident visa after 3 years
R — Resident (Long-Term)After 3 years on M-10 or 5 years of cumulative residencyPermanent residency; starts naturalization clock
M-12 — Real EstateInvestment in real estate worth 350 SMLMV (~$98,000 USD)As of 2024 rate

SMLMV = Salario Mínimo Legal Mensual Vigente (Colombian minimum wage); amount adjusted annually.

Once permanent residency (Visa R) is obtained, the 5-year (or 1-year for Ibero-Americans) naturalization clock applies.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Colombia is South America’s fourth-largest economy (GDP ~$363 billion USD) and one of the region’s fastest-growing tech ecosystems. Medellín has become internationally recognized as a major innovation hub (ranked among the world’s most innovative cities). Bogotá hosts the largest startup scene, with hundreds of accelerators, VCs, and multinational regional headquarters.

Colombia has undergone significant economic transformation since the early 2000s, shifting from a conflict-heavy economy to a diversified services, manufacturing, and technology economy. The country benefits from a young population, competitive wages, growing digital infrastructure, and Pacific Alliance membership.

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

Colombian nationals may renounce citizenship voluntarily by appearing before a notary public or Colombian consulate and executing a formal renunciation instrument. Renunciation is filed with the Registro Civil. Minors cannot renounce nationality.

Involuntary Loss

CauseRule
Acquisition of foreign nationalityDoes NOT cause loss — constitutional guarantee
Renunciation by a minor’s guardianNot permitted — only individuals of majority can renounce
Fraudulent naturalizationCancellation possible by administrative resolution
Serving foreign state during war against ColombiaHistorical provision; not actively enforced

Key point: The 1991 Constitution made involuntary loss of nationality extremely difficult. Colombia does not strip citizenship for voting in foreign elections, serving in foreign governments, or acquiring other passports.

Recovery / Resumption

Colombians who voluntarily renounced nationality may recover it by:

  1. Re-establishing legal domicile in Colombia
  2. Filing a recovery petition with the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores
  3. The recovery process is administrative and does not require the full 5-year naturalization process for original nationals — it is treated as a resumption of prior status

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Valid passport (certified copy of all pages)
  • Cédula de Extranjería (foreigner ID) or valid current visa
  • Proof of legal residence duration (entry/exit stamps, visa renewals, tax declarations, bank statements)
  • Criminal background check from country of origin (apostilled, Spanish translation)
  • Colombian Judicial Background Certificate (Certificado Judicial — Policía Nacional or Fiscalía)
  • Birth certificate (apostilled, Spanish translation)
  • Marriage certificate if claiming spousal track (apostilled, translated)
  • Proof of economic solvency (bank statements, employment contract, tax returns)
  • Passport-size photos (recent)
  • Completed application form (Cancillería)
  • Application fee payment (Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior or bank)
