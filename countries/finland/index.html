Dual Citizenship

Finland fully permits dual and multiple citizenship since the entry into force of the Nationality Act (359/2003) on 1 June 2003.

Current rules:

  • Finnish citizens who acquire a foreign nationality do not lose their Finnish citizenship.
  • Foreign nationals who naturalize as Finnish are not required to renounce their prior nationality.
  • There are no restrictions based on the other country involved.

Pre-2003 rules (for reference):

  • Before 1 June 2003, Finnish citizens who voluntarily acquired a foreign nationality lost their Finnish citizenship automatically upon turning 22 (if they had not resided in Finland or another Nordic country).
  • Naturalization applicants were required to renounce their prior nationality.

Retroactive provision: The 2003 Act included a transitional provision allowing former Finnish citizens who lost their citizenship between 1 June 2003 and 1 June 2008 to re-acquire it by declaration. This window has closed. Current re-acquisition requires standard naturalization.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Finland does not apply a general jus soli principle. Birth on Finnish territory alone does not confer citizenship.

Exceptions (Section 9):

  • A child born in Finland who does not acquire any other nationality at birth acquires Finnish citizenship (statelessness prevention).
  • Foundlings found in Finland are deemed Finnish citizens until another nationality is established.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Finnish citizenship is acquired automatically at birth in the following cases (Sections 6—8):

Mother is a Finnish citizen:

  • The child acquires Finnish citizenship at birth regardless of where the child is born and regardless of the parents’ marital status.

Father is a Finnish citizen:

  • If the parents are married: the child acquires Finnish citizenship at birth, regardless of where born.
  • If the parents are not married and the child is born in Finland: the child acquires Finnish citizenship at birth once paternity is established.
  • If the parents are not married and the child is born abroad: the child acquires Finnish citizenship by declaration (not automatically) after paternity is established.

Generational limit (Section 8(3)):

  • Finnish citizenship is not automatically acquired by a child born abroad if the only link is a Finnish parent who was also born abroad and has never resided in Finland, AND the child would acquire another nationality at birth. This is intended to prevent indefinite transmission through generations with no connection to Finland.

Historical note: Before 1984, Finnish citizenship was transmitted only through the father (for children born in wedlock) or the mother (for children born out of wedlock). Persons affected by this gender discrimination may claim citizenship by declaration.

By Adoption

A child under 12 adopted by a Finnish citizen acquires Finnish citizenship automatically upon the adoption becoming effective under Finnish law (Section 10), provided:

  • The child is under 12 at the time of the adoption.
  • The adoption is recognized under Finnish law (domestically or through the Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption).
  • Children aged 12—17 adopted by Finnish citizens may apply for citizenship by declaration.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Finnish citizen does not automatically confer citizenship. There is no separate spousal naturalization pathway. The foreign spouse must meet the standard naturalization requirements, though the residency period may be calculated more favorably:

Reduced residence for Nordic citizens: Citizens of Sweden, Norway, Denmark, and Iceland may apply after 4 years of continuous residence (or 6 total with 2 continuous), reflecting the Nordic cooperation framework.

Declaration pathway (Section 28—33):

Certain categories may acquire Finnish citizenship by declaration (a simplified right-based process rather than discretionary naturalization):

  • Former Finnish citizens.
  • Persons aged 18—22 who have resided in Finland for at least 6 years (or 10 years with the last 2 continuous) and have no significant criminal record.
  • Children born in Finland to a Finnish father outside of marriage, after paternity is established.
  • Persons adopted as children by Finnish citizens (age 12—17 at adoption).

Loss and Renunciation

Voluntary Renunciation

A Finnish citizen may renounce citizenship by filing a written notification with Migri (Section 35), provided:

  1. They hold another nationality or have been assured one.
  2. They are domiciled outside Finland.
  3. Renunciation will not render them stateless.

If the person is domiciled in Finland, they may still renounce but must demonstrate that they hold another nationality.

Involuntary Loss

GroundDetails
Age 22 rule (Section 34)A Finnish citizen who also holds another nationality and was born abroad, has never resided in Finland, and has no other substantial connection to Finland loses Finnish citizenship at age 22 — unless they apply for retention before that age or loss would render them stateless. This mirrors the Nordic practice of preventing indefinite citizenship transmission.
Acquisition by fraudCitizenship obtained through false information may be revoked within 5 years of discovery (Section 33a).

Important: Finland does not revoke citizenship for voluntary acquisition of a foreign nationality (since 2003). There are no provisions for loss based on foreign military service, disloyalty, or prolonged absence (beyond the age-22 rule).

Restoration of Nationality

Former Finnish citizens who lost their citizenship (by the pre-2003 automatic loss rules, by renunciation, or by the age-22 rule) may re-acquire Finnish citizenship through:

Declaration (Section 28—29):

  • Former Finnish citizens may acquire citizenship by declaration regardless of where they reside.
  • No residency requirement.
  • No language requirement for the declaration pathway.
  • Must not have been convicted of a serious criminal offense.
  • Dual citizenship is permitted.

Standard naturalization:

  • Former citizens who do not qualify for the declaration pathway may apply through the standard naturalization process. Previous Finnish citizenship is considered a favorable factor.

Stateless Persons and Foundlings

  • Foundlings discovered on Finnish territory are deemed Finnish citizens until another nationality is established (Section 9).
  • Stateless children born in Finland who do not acquire any other nationality at birth acquire Finnish citizenship automatically.
  • Stateless persons may apply for naturalization with reduced residence requirements (4 years continuous or 6 total) at the discretion of Migri.
  • Finland is a party to the 1954 Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness.

Document Checklist

  • Application form for Finnish citizenship (filed online via Enter Finland or on paper at Migri service point)
  • Valid passport and Finnish residence permit
  • Birth certificate (apostilled + certified Finnish, Swedish, or English translation)
  • Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  • Finnish or Swedish language proficiency certificate (YKI level 3, or equivalent proof)
  • Proof of continuous lawful residence (population register extract from DVV — Digital and Population Data Services Agency)
  • Proof of income / livelihood (employment contract, tax decisions, Kela records)
  • Criminal record certificate from country of origin (apostilled + translated)
  • Proof of identity (valid passport or other reliable identity document)
  • Biometric passport photos
  • Fee payment receipt (EUR 460 online / EUR 690 paper)
