Dual Citizenship

Trinidad and Tobago permits dual citizenship under the Trinidad and Tobago Citizenship Act. The country has long maintained a liberal stance on dual nationality, in recognition of the large Trinidadian and Tobagonian diaspora communities in the United Kingdom, the United States, and Canada. Citizens who acquire foreign nationalities do not automatically lose their Trinidad and Tobago citizenship, and foreign nationals who naturalize in Trinidad and Tobago are not required to renounce their prior citizenship under Trinidadian law.

The Trinidad and Tobago passport is one of the most powerful in the Caribbean and Latin America, granting visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 143 countries as of 2025. This includes the entire Schengen Area, the United Kingdom, Singapore, Hong Kong, Brazil, and most of Latin America. The United States requires a visa for Trinidadian passport holders, though the country has been in ongoing discussions regarding the Visa Waiver Program. Trinidad and Tobago is a Commonwealth member, conferring certain reciprocal privileges in other Commonwealth nations.

The strength of the Trinidad and Tobago passport relative to its Caribbean neighbors is partly a function of its economic size — as the largest economy in the English-speaking Caribbean and a major hydrocarbon exporter, it maintains a wide network of diplomatic relations and bilateral agreements. This makes Trinidadian citizenship particularly attractive to regional investors and professionals seeking a high-travel-access Caribbean base.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

A person born in Trinidad and Tobago is a citizen by birth if at least one parent is a citizen of Trinidad and Tobago or is lawfully and permanently resident in the country at the time of birth. Children born to foreign diplomatic staff on assignment are excluded. The jus soli principle is qualified — birth alone without at least one qualifying parent does not confer citizenship, consistent with the 1962 Independence Constitution and subsequent amendments.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

A person born outside Trinidad and Tobago is a citizen by descent if at least one parent is a Trinidad and Tobago citizen at the time of the child’s birth. Citizenship by descent extends to the first generation born abroad; subsequent generations typically require registration within a set timeframe or through consular services. Registration of citizenship by descent is handled by the Immigration Division of the Ministry of National Security.

By Adoption

A child who is adopted by a Trinidad and Tobago citizen may be registered as a citizen. The adoption must be legally recognized under Trinidadian law or under a foreign law that is recognized in Trinidad and Tobago. Applications are processed through the Citizenship Unit of the Ministry of National Security.

By Marriage

A foreign national married to a citizen of Trinidad and Tobago may apply for citizenship by registration. The marriage must be legally valid and subsisting. The applicant must have been ordinarily resident in Trinidad and Tobago for a period of time (typically at least 5 years of lawful residence is expected in practice, though the law provides for registration after marriage with the Minister’s approval). Genuine marital relationship, good character, and intent to remain are required. Applicants must take an oath of allegiance.

By Naturalization (Residency)

The standard naturalization path requires 8 years of lawful and ordinary residence in Trinidad and Tobago immediately preceding the application. For citizens of Commonwealth countries, the period is reduced to 5 years. Applicants must:

  • Have been physically present in Trinidad and Tobago throughout the qualifying period (limited absences may be permitted at the Minister’s discretion)
  • Be of good character (criminal background check, police certificate from all countries of prior residence)
  • Have an adequate knowledge of the English language
  • Intend to remain ordinarily resident in Trinidad and Tobago
  • Take an oath of allegiance to the Republic

Applications are made to the Citizenship Unit, Ministry of National Security, 12–14 Abercromby Street, Port of Spain. The Minister has discretionary authority to waive or modify requirements in exceptional cases, including persons of distinguished record, those who have made significant contributions to national development, or cases of hardship.

Citizenship / Residency by Investment

Trinidad and Tobago does not operate a formal Citizenship by Investment (CBI) program. Unlike several of its OECS neighbors, there is no published route to acquire citizenship through a direct financial contribution to a national development fund or real estate purchase.

The primary investment facilitation body is InvesTT (Investment in Trinidad and Tobago), the national investment promotion agency. InvesTT assists foreign investors in identifying business opportunities, navigating regulatory approvals, and accessing investment incentives. It does not administer immigration directly.

Pathways for investors and business persons:

  • Business Person Permit: Foreign nationals wishing to operate a business in Trinidad and Tobago may apply for a business person permit from the Immigration Division. These permits are renewable and, when maintained lawfully over a sufficient period (8 years for non-Commonwealth nationals; 5 years for Commonwealth nationals), form the basis of a naturalization application.
  • Employment Permits: Foreign professionals employed by local companies or their own incorporated entities may obtain employment permits, which similarly count toward the residency requirement.
  • Ministerial Discretion: The Minister of National Security has broad discretion to grant citizenship to persons who have made exceptional contributions to the country’s economic, cultural, or scientific development.

Investors interested in the energy sector, financial services, or manufacturing should consult with InvesTT (https://www.investt.co.tt) and engage a licensed Trinidadian immigration attorney.

Trinidad and Tobago taxes residents on worldwide income. Non-residents are taxed on Trinidad-source income only. There is no capital gains tax. The country has double taxation treaties with Canada, the United Kingdom, the United States, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Switzerland, Venezuela, China, India, and several CARICOM states.

The energy sector — oil, gas, and petrochemicals — is subject to a separate fiscal regime including the Petroleum Profits Tax and the Supplemental Petroleum Tax, administered by the Inland Revenue Division.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

The banking sector is well-developed by Caribbean standards. Major institutions include Republic Bank (the largest indigenous bank in the Caribbean by assets), First Citizens Bank, Scotiabank Trinidad and Tobago, RBC Royal Bank, and JMMB Trinidad and Tobago. The Central Bank of Trinidad and Tobago (https://www.central-bank.org.tt) supervises the financial sector.

USD-denominated accounts are available. Correspondent banking relationships with major US and UK institutions are maintained. The Trinidad and Tobago Stock Exchange (TTSE) provides equity market access. The country’s financial centre in Port of Spain hosts a number of regional and international financial services firms.

Account opening for foreign nationals requires passport, proof of address, source of funds documentation, and reference letters. Compliance with FATF and CFATF standards means enhanced due diligence for applicants from higher-risk jurisdictions.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Energy Sector Fiscal Regime: Deeply competitive fiscal terms for upstream oil and gas, managed by the Ministry of Energy and Energy Industries and the National Gas Company of Trinidad and Tobago (NGC).
  • InvesTT Investment Incentives: Facilitation for manufacturing (customs duty concessions, tax holidays up to 10 years under the Fiscal Incentives Act), tourism, agro-processing, and ICT.
  • Free Trade Zones: The Free Zones Act provides for designated free zones offering duty-free importation and relief from corporate income tax on export earnings.
  • CARICOM Single Market: As a founding CARICOM member, Trinidad and Tobago provides access to the CARICOM Single Market and Economy (CSME), enabling free movement of goods, services, capital, and skilled labor within the region.
  • Point Lisas Industrial Estate: The world-class Point Lisas estate hosts major energy-based industries including ammonia, methanol, and steel, with supporting infrastructure and logistics.

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A citizen of Trinidad and Tobago who holds or is about to acquire the citizenship of another country may renounce their Trinidad and Tobago citizenship by making a formal declaration before a prescribed authority. The renunciation must be registered with the Citizenship Unit and takes effect upon registration. A renouncing citizen who later wishes to reacquire citizenship must go through the naturalization process.

Involuntary Loss

The Citizenship Act provides for deprivation of citizenship in specific circumstances:

  • Citizenship obtained by fraud, false representation, or deliberate concealment of material facts
  • Disloyalty or disaffection toward the Republic (e.g., actions in support of an enemy during wartime)
  • Conviction of an offence under certain laws of Trinidad and Tobago within a specified period of naturalization
  • Residence abroad for a continuous period of seven or more years (this provision applies mainly to citizens by descent who fail to register their intention to retain citizenship)

Deprivation requires a formal inquiry and ministerial order. The state is prohibited from rendering a person stateless.

Recovery / Resumption

Former citizens who lost citizenship through renunciation or who were deprived of citizenship may apply for re-admission to citizenship through naturalization, subject to the standard residence and good character requirements. Those deprived on grounds of fraud or national security face additional scrutiny. The Minister retains discretion in such cases.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Valid passport (current and all prior passports)
  • Original birth certificate (certified copy acceptable)
  • Police clearance certificate from Trinidad and Tobago and all countries of prior residence (within the last 5 years)
  • Proof of continuous lawful residence (immigration stamp records, work permits, employer letters, utility bills — covering the full qualifying period)
  • Proof of employment or business operation in Trinidad and Tobago
  • Evidence of economic and social integration (bank statements, lease agreements, community involvement)
  • Certificate of marriage (if applying as spouse of citizen)
  • Four passport-sized photographs (recent, color)
  • Completed citizenship application form (Ministry of National Security)
  • Two character reference letters from respected citizens of Trinidad and Tobago
  • Medical certificate of good health
  • Application fee payment
