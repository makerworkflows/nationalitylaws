Dual Citizenship

The Bahamas has permitted dual citizenship since independence in 1973. The Bahamian Nationality Act contains no prohibition on holding foreign nationalities simultaneously, and there is no requirement to renounce a prior nationality upon naturalization. Bahamian citizens who acquire a second nationality abroad are also not required to surrender their Bahamian passport.

The Bahamian passport is one of the strongest issued by any Caribbean nation. It provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 155 destinations, including the full Schengen Area, the United Kingdom, and the majority of the world’s economies. This makes Bahamian nationality genuinely valuable beyond its domestic context — access to Europe without a visa is a significant differentiator for a small island nation.

There is no formal citizenship by investment program in The Bahamas. The government has deliberately avoided establishing a CBI scheme, positioning the country as a premium destination rather than a passport-sale jurisdiction. As a result, nationality is acquired through the traditional channels of birth, descent, marriage, or long-term residence. The investment options that exist — discussed below — relate to residency permits, not direct citizenship.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

A person born in The Bahamas to at least one Bahamian citizen parent acquires Bahamian citizenship at birth. Pure jus soli (birthright citizenship for children born to two non-citizen parents in Bahamian territory) does not apply automatically. Children born in The Bahamas to two non-citizen parents may register as citizens upon reaching the age of 18 if they have lived there continuously and are of good character.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Citizenship by descent is available to persons born outside The Bahamas to a Bahamian citizen father (for those born in wedlock) or a Bahamian citizen mother (for those born out of wedlock, or born in wedlock after 1 August 1973). Second-generation and beyond descent claims become more complex and generally require registration within specific time windows after birth or reaching adulthood.

Children born abroad to a Bahamian parent should be registered at the nearest Bahamian mission or the Department of Immigration in Nassau as early as possible to establish nationality formally.

By Adoption

A child lawfully adopted by a Bahamian citizen acquires the right to register as a Bahamian citizen. The adoption must be legally recognized under Bahamian law. The process involves an application to the Department of Immigration and is generally straightforward for children adopted through recognized legal channels.

By Marriage

A foreign national who marries a Bahamian citizen does not acquire citizenship automatically. However, they may apply for naturalization on an accelerated basis after five years of lawful residence in The Bahamas as the spouse of a Bahamian citizen. Standard naturalization criteria apply: good character, basic English language ability (the country is English-speaking), and demonstration of genuine ties to the country. The spousal pathway reduces the standard residency requirement from ten years to five.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Standard naturalization requires ten years of lawful ordinary residence in The Bahamas. The applicant must:

  • Have resided lawfully and continuously (with permitted absences) for at least ten years immediately before application
  • Be of good character (criminal record check; character references required)
  • Intend to continue residing in The Bahamas
  • Take an oath of allegiance to the Commonwealth of The Bahamas

There is no formal English language test since English is the de facto and de jure language of the country, and all applicants are expected to demonstrate functional English through the application and interview process. There is no mandatory civics exam, though applicants may be questioned on their knowledge of the country during an interview with the Department of Immigration.

The naturalization process is reviewed by a committee and ultimately approved by the Governor-General on the advice of the Minister responsible for immigration. Processing times historically range from 12 to 24 months, and in some cases longer depending on application volume and background check complexity.

Citizenship / Residency by Investment

The Bahamas does not operate a formal Citizenship by Investment (CBI) program. The government has on multiple occasions declined to create one, citing concerns about reputational risk and a preference for maintaining the country’s standing as a quality financial and tourism jurisdiction rather than a passport-issuing jurisdiction.

Extended Access and Stay (EAS) Permit

The primary investment-linked residence mechanism is the Extended Access and Stay (EAS) permit, formerly marketed under various names. This is a long-stay permit designed for investors, retirees, and high-net-worth individuals who wish to spend extended periods in The Bahamas without undertaking employment.

Key characteristics of the EAS:

  • Intended for investors, retirees, and persons of independent means
  • Applicants must demonstrate a real estate investment or significant financial ties to The Bahamas
  • Real estate investment threshold: typically USD $750,000 or more in Bahamian property qualifies for expedited consideration, though no fixed minimum is published for all categories
  • Permit is renewable and does not automatically convert to permanent residence or citizenship
  • Holders may not work for Bahamian entities without a separate work permit

Annual Residence (Permanent) Permit

After accumulating ten years of lawful residence — which may include years on EAS permits — a foreign national may apply for an Annual (Permanent) Residence permit. This is not the same as citizenship but provides indefinite right of residence. Only after an appropriate period of lawful permanent residence does the ten-year naturalization clock generally begin to run for practical purposes.

High Elective Residence Program

Separately, the High Elective Residence Program targets retirees with demonstrable passive income. Applicants must demonstrate a monthly income of at least BSD $2,000 (approximately USD $2,000 given the peg) from pensions, investments, or other passive sources. No minimum investment in real estate is mandated, though many applicants hold property. This permit is renewable annually and does not create a direct pathway to citizenship independent of the ten-year residency rule.

No Passport-Sale Mechanism

It must be clearly stated: there is no mechanism in Bahamian law by which a financial contribution or investment directly results in citizenship. Any service claiming to sell a Bahamian passport through investment should be treated as fraudulent. Bahamian nationality requires genuine physical residency over time.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Company Formation

The Bahamas is an internationally recognized business jurisdiction. The primary vehicle for foreign investors is the International Business Company (IBC), governed by the International Business Companies Act. Key characteristics:

  • Fast incorporation (24–72 hours in most cases)
  • No requirement to file accounts publicly
  • Flexible corporate structures; single-member allowed
  • No minimum capital requirement for most structures
  • Business Activity License required for domestic commerce

For domestic businesses operating within The Bahamas, registration with the Registrar General and obtaining the relevant Business License under the Business License Act are mandatory.

The Bahamas has signed a number of Tax Information Exchange Agreements (TIEAs) with the United States, Canada, and EU member states, and it is not blacklisted by the OECD or EU for non-cooperative tax jurisdictions, having committed to the BEPS standards and automatic exchange of information (AEOI) under the Common Reporting Standard (CRS).

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

The Bahamas hosts one of the most developed financial sectors in the Caribbean. Nassau is home to numerous licensed banks and trust companies, both domestic and international. The country’s financial services regulatory framework is overseen by the Securities Commission of The Bahamas (SCB) and the Central Bank of The Bahamas.

The Bahamian dollar (BSD) is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar, which eliminates exchange rate risk for USD-denominated transactions and simplifies banking for American clients. Major international banks operating in The Bahamas include institutions from the United States, Canada, and Switzerland, alongside regional Caribbean banks.

The Bahamas was an early adopter of digital currency infrastructure — the Sand Dollar, the world’s first government-issued central bank digital currency (CBDC) at national scale, was launched in October 2020 by the Central Bank of The Bahamas.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Industries Encouragement Act: Exempts approved manufacturing industries from import duties on raw materials and customs duties for a defined period
  • Hotels Encouragement Act: Tax exemptions for hotel construction and renovation
  • Export Manufacturing Industries Encouragement Act: Incentives for export-oriented manufacturers
  • Financial and Corporate Service Providers Act: Framework for fintech and digital asset businesses

The Bahamas passed the Digital Assets and Registered Exchanges (DARE) Act in 2020, establishing a licensing regime for digital asset businesses and making The Bahamas one of the first jurisdictions in the Caribbean to regulate crypto exchanges and custodians under a comprehensive legal framework.

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A Bahamian citizen of full age may renounce their Bahamian citizenship by making a declaration of renunciation to the Minister responsible for immigration. Renunciation is generally permitted but may be withheld during periods of armed conflict or if the Minister determines it would be contrary to public interest. Once accepted, the declaration is registered and the person ceases to be a Bahamian citizen.

Involuntary Loss

Bahamian citizenship acquired by naturalization or registration may be revoked by the Governor-General if the citizenship was obtained by fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment of material facts. Citizenship may also be lost if a naturalized citizen acts in a manner contrary to the interests of The Bahamas — for example, serving in the armed forces of a foreign state with which The Bahamas is at war, or engaging in treasonous conduct. Citizenship by birth cannot be revoked on these grounds.

Recovery / Resumption

A former Bahamian citizen who lost citizenship through voluntary renunciation or through marriage (under older laws prior to amendments) may apply to resume citizenship. The process involves an application to the Department of Immigration and is subject to the discretion of the Minister. Resumption is not guaranteed and depends on circumstances, character, and ties to the country.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For naturalization, the following are typically required:

  • Completed application form (Form N-1 or current version)
  • Valid passport and all previous passports showing entry/exit stamps
  • Birth certificate (certified/apostilled)
  • Marriage certificate if applicable
  • Police clearance certificates from The Bahamas and all countries of residence in the past ten years
  • Character references (typically two Bahamian citizens)
  • Evidence of lawful residence (immigration permits, employer letters, tax records)
  • Medical certificate
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Application fee (BSD $25 application; BSD $500 upon grant)

Where to Submit

Applications are submitted to:

Department of Immigration Manx Corporate Centre West Bay Street Nassau, New Providence, The Bahamas

For applicants residing outside Nassau, submissions may be made through the nearest local government office or Bahamian High Commission/Embassy.

Processing Time

Naturalization applications are reviewed by the Nationality Committee and forwarded to the Governor-General for final approval. Typical processing: 12–24 months. Applicants with complex backgrounds or those requiring additional background checks may experience longer timelines.
