Dual Citizenship

Yes — El Salvador recognizes dual and multiple nationality. The 1983 Constitution of the Republic of El Salvador does not require renunciation of prior nationality upon acquiring Salvadoran citizenship, nor does it revoke Salvadoran nationality for citizens who acquire a foreign nationality.

Constitutional basis: Article 90 of the Constitution establishes that Salvadorans by birth retain their nationality even if they acquire citizenship of another country. Article 91 addresses naturalized citizens and similarly does not impose a loss of nationality for acquiring foreign citizenship.

El Salvador is notably permissive on dual citizenship. Unlike some Latin American countries that only introduced dual citizenship protections in the 1990s or 2000s, El Salvador’s 1983 Constitution embedded this principle from the outset. Salvadorans living abroad (a massive diaspora, particularly in the United States) have always been able to maintain their Salvadoran nationality regardless of naturalizing elsewhere.

Key note for the diaspora: An estimated 2.5+ million Salvadorans live in the United States. Salvadoran nationality by birth is never lost, meaning U.S.-naturalized Salvadorans remain Salvadoran nationals and can obtain Salvadoran passports, vote in Salvadoran elections, and transmit nationality to their children.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

El Salvador applies unrestricted jus soli. Any person born on Salvadoran territory acquires Salvadoran nationality by birth, regardless of the nationality of their parents.

Constitutional basis: Article 90(1) of the Constitution of the Republic of El Salvador.

This includes:

  • Children born anywhere within Salvadoran national territory (including territorial waters and airspace)
  • Children born on Salvadoran-registered vessels or aircraft while in international waters or airspace

Additionally, Article 90(3) extends jus soli nationality to children of a Salvadoran father or mother born in the territory of the former Federal Republic of Central America (the historical federation comprising El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Costa Rica). This reflects El Salvador’s deep commitment to Central American unity.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Salvadoran nationality by birth extends to children born abroad to Salvadoran parents:

ScenarioRequirementConstitutional Basis
Born abroad to a Salvadoran father or motherAt least one parent is Salvadoran national by birthArt. 90(2)
Born abroad in Central American territory to Salvadoran parentsAt least one parent is SalvadoranArt. 90(3)
Born abroad — child of naturalized SalvadoranMay register for nationality; some restrictions may applyArt. 90, Ley de Extranjeria

Registration requirement: Children born abroad to Salvadoran parents must be registered at a Salvadoran consulate. There is no deadline for registration, and adults who discover their claim to Salvadoran nationality by descent may file at any age.

Generational transmission: The Constitution does not impose an explicit generational limit. A child born abroad to a Salvadoran-born-abroad parent can claim nationality, provided the chain of Salvadoran parentage is documented.

By Adoption

Adoption does not automatically confer Salvadoran nationality. However:

  • A foreign child adopted by Salvadoran nationals may be registered for Salvadoran nationality if the adoption is legally recognized in El Salvador
  • Minor children adopted before age 18 generally qualify for nationality registration through their adoptive Salvadoran parents
  • Adult adoptees would need to pursue naturalization through standard or reduced-residency channels

By Marriage

Marriage to a Salvadoran national does not automatically confer nationality. However, the Constitution provides a facilitated path:

RequirementDetail
Residency1 year of legal residence in El Salvador (if spouse is Salvadoran by birth)

Good conductClean criminal record required

Constitutional basis: Article 92(3) provides that foreigners married to Salvadorans by birth who have resided in the country for two or more years prior to and at the time of application may naturalize. In practice, the marriage category is often combined with the Central American or Ibero-American preference, which can reduce the requirement to one year.

Foreign nationals who have resided legally in El Salvador may apply for naturalization (carta de naturalizacion).

Note on Central American preferential treatment: Article 92(1) of the Constitution is remarkably generous — nationals of Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, and Costa Rica need only one year of residence to naturalize in El Salvador. This reflects the historical ties of the former Federal Republic of Central America and the ongoing CA-4 free movement agreement.

CA-4 Free Movement Zone

El Salvador is a member of the CA-4 (Central America-4) free movement agreement alongside Guatemala, Honduras, and Nicaragua. Key provisions:

FeatureDetail
Free movementCitizens of CA-4 countries may move freely across borders without passports (national ID card sufficient)
No visa requiredUnlimited stays for CA-4 nationals
Right to workFacilitated work permits for CA-4 nationals
NaturalizationOnly 1 year of residency required for citizenship
Historical basisThese four countries plus Costa Rica formed the Federal Republic of Central America (1823-1841)

The CA-4 agreement was formalized in June 2006 and represents one of the most integrated free movement zones in the Americas. For practical purposes, a Guatemalan, Honduran, or Nicaraguan citizen can relocate to El Salvador, live and work there with minimal bureaucratic friction, and apply for naturalization after just one year of residence.

Special Clauses & Programs

Central American Union Provisions

El Salvador’s Constitution contains some of the strongest Central American unionist language of any country in the region. Article 89 states that El Salvador will promote the reconstruction of the Central American Republic “in any form, unitary, federal, or confederate,” and empowers the executive to implement agreements toward political, economic, and social integration with Central American states without requiring a new constitutional process. This ideological commitment directly shapes nationality law — the preferential 1-year naturalization for Central Americans is a constitutional expression of this vision.

Nationality for Service to the Republic

The Constitution and secondary legislation allow for expedited or waived residency requirements for foreigners who have rendered notable services to El Salvador in cultural, scientific, industrial, commercial, or other domains of national benefit. This is granted by legislative decree on a case-by-case basis.

Nationality for Industrial/Enterprise Founders

Article 92(2) provides a specific preference for foreigners who have resided in El Salvador for one or more years and have established new industries or enterprises of notable utility. The combination of enterprise creation and reduced residency creates an entrepreneur-friendly pathway to citizenship.

Honorary Nationality

The Asamblea Legislativa (Legislative Assembly) may confer honorary Salvadoran nationality to foreign nationals who have provided exceptional service to the nation. This is rare and ceremonial.

No Traditional Citizenship by Investment Program

El Salvador does not offer a conventional citizenship-by-investment (CBI) or “golden passport” program in the Caribbean or Malta model. However, the Bitcoin-related residency programs (see Investment Pathways below) and the enterprise-creation provisions in Article 92(2) provide investment-adjacent pathways.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Not available in the traditional sense. El Salvador does not offer a direct cash-for-passport program. However, the enterprise-creation preference in Article 92(2) of the Constitution provides a pathway where foreign nationals who establish industries or enterprises of notable utility may qualify for reduced residency (as little as 1 year) before naturalization.

Bitcoin Freedom Visa / Residency Programs

El Salvador made global headlines in September 2021 by becoming the first country in the world to adopt Bitcoin as legal tender (alongside the U.S. dollar) through the Bitcoin Law (Ley Bitcoin). Building on this, El Salvador has introduced several programs aimed at attracting crypto investors and digital entrepreneurs:

Freedom Visa details: The Freedom Visa program, announced by President Nayib Bukele’s government, is aimed at attracting global investors willing to make a $1 million contribution (in Bitcoin or Tether) to El Salvador. In exchange, participants receive permanent residency and an expedited path to Salvadoran citizenship. The program is explicitly designed to grow the country’s Bitcoin reserves and attract high-net-worth individuals who share El Salvador’s vision for cryptocurrency adoption.

Important caveats:

  • Program terms and availability may shift as El Salvador’s Bitcoin strategy evolves
  • The regulatory framework is still maturing — prospective applicants should verify current terms directly with the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores or authorized immigration attorneys
  • El Salvador’s Bitcoin adoption has been met with both enthusiasm from the crypto community and caution from international financial institutions (such as the IMF)

Business & Entrepreneurship

El Salvador has positioned itself as one of the most crypto-forward nations on Earth. Under President Nayib Bukele, the country has aggressively pursued foreign investment through Bitcoin adoption, tax incentives, and a streamlined business environment. The dollarized economy (El Salvador has used the U.S. dollar as its official currency since 2001, now alongside Bitcoin) removes currency risk for North American and European investors. Security improvements under the state of exception have dramatically reduced crime, making the country more attractive for business. However, the small domestic market (6.5 million people), developing infrastructure outside San Salvador, and institutional risks temper the overall picture.

Company Formation

Most common entity typeSociedad Anonima de Capital Variable (S.A. de C.V.)
Formation timeline2-4 weeks
Formation cost~$800-$2,000 USD (including legal, registration, and notary fees)
Minimum capital$2,000 USD (11,400 colones equivalent; paid in USD)
Online registrationPartial — Centro Nacional de Registros (CNR) handles registration; some steps available online via MiEmpresa.gob.sv

The S.A. de C.V. (variable capital corporation) is the most common structure for foreign investors and medium-to-large businesses. The S. de R.L. (limited liability company) is available for smaller operations. Formation requires a Salvadoran notary (notario), registration with the Centro Nacional de Registros (CNR), registration with the Ministerio de Hacienda (tax authority), and enrollment in the social security system (ISSS) and pension system (AFP).

Foreign ownership: Foreign nationals may own 100% of a Salvadoran company. There are no general restrictions on foreign ownership, though certain sectors (mining, some natural resources) may have regulatory requirements. El Salvador’s Investment Law (Ley de Inversiones) guarantees equal treatment for foreign and domestic investors.

Single-window registration: The government has introduced MiEmpresa (mi empresa = my company), a single-window system intended to streamline company formation by consolidating multiple registrations into one process.

Entrepreneur & Startup Visas

El Salvador does not have a dedicated “startup visa” in the formal sense, but several pathways serve entrepreneurs:

  • Temporary Resident Visa (Residente Temporal): Allows engagement in business activities; renewable annually for up to 5 years; requires proof of income or economic activity
  • Investor Visa: For foreigners establishing or investing in Salvadoran businesses; requires proof of investment and a viable business plan
  • Freedom Visa: For high-net-worth individuals making a $1 million Bitcoin/USDT investment (see Investment Pathways above)
  • Digital Nomad Program: For remote workers earning $1,450+/month from foreign sources; 2-year duration with tax exemptions on foreign-sourced income
  • Enterprise-Based Naturalization: Article 92(2) of the Constitution offers reduced residency requirements for foreigners who establish new industries or enterprises of notable utility — effectively an entrepreneur-to-citizenship pathway

Bitcoin legal tender status: El Salvador’s Ley Bitcoin (Bitcoin Law), enacted June 8, 2021 and effective September 7, 2021, makes Bitcoin legal tender alongside the U.S. dollar. All businesses are required to accept Bitcoin as payment (though practical enforcement varies). The government launched the Chivo Wallet for Bitcoin transactions and installed Bitcoin ATMs nationwide.

Tax exemptions for Bitcoin investors: In a major move to attract crypto capital, El Salvador eliminated capital gains taxes and income taxes on Bitcoin profits for foreign nationals. This makes it one of the most tax-favorable jurisdictions in the world for cryptocurrency holders.

Territorial taxation notes: El Salvador generally taxes on a worldwide basis for residents. However, the Bitcoin tax exemption and digital nomad program carve out significant exceptions for foreign-sourced income and cryptocurrency gains. Prospective residents should consult a Salvadoran tax advisor for current structuring options.

Free trade zones: El Salvador operates free trade zones (zonas francas) offering significant tax incentives:

  • 100% exemption from income tax for up to 20 years
  • 100% exemption from import duties on raw materials and equipment
  • 100% exemption from municipal taxes
  • Available for manufacturing, services, and call center operations

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

  • Major banks: Banco Agricola (largest, Bancolombia subsidiary), Banco Cuscatlan (Grupo Terra), Banco Davivienda, Scotiabank El Salvador, Banco de America Central (BAC Credomatic)
  • Digital banks/fintech: Chivo Wallet (government Bitcoin wallet), Strike, Flexa; fintech ecosystem is growing but still early-stage
  • Currency: U.S. Dollar (official since 2001) + Bitcoin (legal tender since 2021)
  • Stripe: Not yet available in El Salvador (as of 2026)
  • SWIFT/wire transfers: Available through all major banks
  • Banking access for foreigners: Requires valid residency card (carnet de residente) or DUI (national ID for citizens); some banks accept passport for basic accounts
  • Remittances: El Salvador receives ~$8 billion annually in remittances (primarily from the U.S.), representing approximately 25% of GDP — one of the highest ratios in the world
  • Bitcoin infrastructure: 200+ Chivo ATMs nationwide, Bitcoin-accepting businesses (especially in El Zonte / “Bitcoin Beach”), government-promoted Bitcoin bond (Volcano Bond) initiatives

Key Programs & Incentives

  • PROESA (Export and Investment Promotion Agency of El Salvador) — The primary government agency for attracting foreign investment; provides advisory services, site selection, and aftercare for foreign companies establishing operations
  • Free Trade Zones (Zonas Francas) — Up to 20-year tax holidays for qualifying companies (manufacturing, services, technology, call centers)
  • CONAMYPE — National Commission for Micro and Small Enterprises; provides training, financing, and support for small businesses
  • BANDESAL — Development Bank of El Salvador; offers financing for productive investment and entrepreneurship
  • Bitcoin Beach (El Zonte) — The original Bitcoin circular economy community; now a global reference point for grassroots Bitcoin adoption
  • Surf City initiative — Government tourism and investment campaign promoting El Salvador’s Pacific coastline for tourism, hospitality, and real estate development
  • San Salvador — The capital and primary business hub; modern infrastructure, international airport (SAL), and growing tech scene
  • Antiguo Cuscatlan / Santa Tecla — Upscale business districts adjacent to San Salvador; popular with foreign entrepreneurs and digital nomads
  • Volcano Bond (Bitcoin Bond) — Proposed sovereign bond backed by Bitcoin revenue and geothermal energy; intended to fund “Bitcoin City,” a planned special economic zone at the base of Conchagua volcano

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

  • Nationals by birth: May voluntarily renounce Salvadoran nationality, but this is extremely rare and discouraged. In practice, El Salvador makes renunciation difficult for birth nationals.
  • Naturalized citizens: May voluntarily renounce by formal declaration before the appropriate authority.

Involuntary Loss

Nationals by birth: Under the 1983 Constitution, Salvadoran nationality acquired by birth is extremely robust. Article 90 does not provide for involuntary loss of nationality by birth. Acquiring a foreign citizenship does not cause loss of Salvadoran nationality.

Naturalized citizens may lose their Salvadoran nationality in the following cases (Article 94 of the Constitution):

CauseDetail
Residing in the country of origin for 2+ consecutive yearsContinuous residence of 2+ years in the country of previous nationality (without authorization)
Absence from El Salvador for 5+ consecutive yearsStaying outside of El Salvador for more than 5 continuous years (rebuttable; exceptions for those in official service or with authorized reasons)
FraudIf the naturalization was obtained through false information, fraudulent documents, or deception
Acts against the stateEngaging in activities deemed contrary to the security or interests of El Salvador
Constitutional or legal groundsOther grounds specified by law

Note: The asymmetry between birth nationals and naturalized citizens is deliberate. Salvadorans by birth enjoy virtually irrevocable nationality, while naturalized citizens face stricter requirements regarding continued ties to El Salvador.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For naturalization by residency (standard checklist):

  • Completed naturalization application form
  • Valid passport (original + certified copy)
  • Current Salvadoran residency card (carnet de residente) — original + copy
  • Birth certificate (original, apostilled or legalized, translated into Spanish by a certified translator)
  • Criminal background check from country of origin (apostilled, translated, recent — typically less than 6 months old)
  • Criminal background check from El Salvador (issued by the Policia Nacional Civil or relevant authority)
  • Medical certificate / health clearance
  • Two or more passport-sized photographs (white background, front-facing)
  • Marriage certificate (if applying under marriage category — apostilled, translated)
  • Spouse’s Salvadoran nationality documentation (if applying under marriage category)
  • Proof of financial solvency (bank statements, employment letter, business registration)
  • Proof of continuous residence in El Salvador (utility bills, lease agreement, employment records)
  • Payment receipt for application fee
  • Letter of intent / declaration of desire to become Salvadoran

For registration of nationality by descent (born abroad to Salvadoran parents):

  • Birth certificate of the applicant (apostilled, translated)
  • Birth certificate of the Salvadoran parent(s) (proving Salvadoran nationality)
  • Salvadoran parent’s DUI or passport
  • Valid foreign passport or ID of the applicant
  • Two passport-sized photographs
