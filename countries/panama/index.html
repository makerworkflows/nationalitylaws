Dual Citizenship

Panama grants full recognition of dual nationality. There is no requirement to renounce a prior citizenship upon acquiring Panamanian nationality, and Panamanians who acquire a foreign citizenship do not automatically lose their Panamanian nationality. This liberal stance makes Panama an attractive destination for those seeking a second passport without sacrificing their original nationality.

The legal basis is found in the Political Constitution of the Republic of Panama (1972, as amended), particularly Articles 8–11, and the Organic Law of the National Migration Service (Law 3 of 2008).

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Panama applies an unconditional jus soli principle with narrow exceptions. Any person born on Panamanian territory is a Panamanian national by birth, except:

  • Children born to foreign diplomatic agents accredited to Panama
  • Children born to foreign persons in transit (defined as a stay of fewer than 30 days)

This broad application of jus soli is embedded in Article 8 of the Panamanian Constitution, making Panama one of the more permissive jurisdictions in the region for birthright citizenship.

Reference: Constitución Política de la República de Panamá, Art. 8(1).

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Children born abroad to at least one Panamanian parent (by birth or naturalization) are entitled to Panamanian nationality by descent.

ScenarioEligibility
One Panamanian parent (either father or mother)Yes — right to register
Both parents PanamanianYes — automatic entitlement
Born abroad to naturalized Panamanian parentYes — eligible
Born abroad to Panamanian grandparent (skipped generation)Requires petition, not automatic

The registration of a child born abroad is handled at the Registro Civil (Civil Registry) of the Tribunal Electoral de Panamá. No age limit is specified for jus sanguinis claims, though registration at birth is encouraged. Adults claiming descent must provide a complete documentary chain.

Reference: Constitución Política, Art. 8(2); Código de la Familia.

By Adoption

Foreign minors legally adopted by Panamanian nationals may acquire Panamanian nationality. The process requires:

  • Final adoption decree issued by a Panamanian court
  • Application to the Civil Registry (Tribunal Electoral)
  • Proof that the adoptive parent(s) are Panamanian nationals

Adopted children are treated equivalently to biological children for purposes of nationality law. Adoption by a naturalized Panamanian parent confers the same rights as adoption by a native-born Panamanian.

By Marriage

Foreign nationals married to Panamanian citizens may apply for naturalization after a reduced residency period.

RequirementDetail

Marriage to a Panamanian national does not automatically confer citizenship — the reduced residency period (3 years versus the standard 5) is the benefit granted by the law.

Reference: Constitución Política, Art. 10; Law 3 of 2008, Art. 32.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Standard Requirements

Any foreign national who has maintained lawful permanent residence in Panama for the statutory period may apply for naturalization.

Reduced Residency Categories

Panama grants reduced naturalization periods for specific categories of foreign nationals:

CategoryReduced Period
Nationals of Ibero-American countries (Spain + Latin America)3 years of residence
Nationals of Spain3 years of residence
Foreign national married to a Panamanian3 years after marriage
Parents of Panamanian-born childrenEligible to apply after legal residence established
Distinguished persons (scientists, academics, artists)Discretionary — may be fast-tracked by decree
Refugees recognized under Panamanian law5 years (standard)

The Ibero-American reduced period reflects Panama’s historical and cultural ties to Spain and the Latin American community. This is one of the most significant reduced-term provisions in the region.

Reference: Constitución Política, Art. 10; Law 3 of 2008.

Preferred Pathways

Panama’s most attractive pathways combine residency-to-citizenship with its world-class residency visa programs:

  1. Friendly Nations Visa → Permanent Residency → Naturalization: Nationals of 50+ countries (including the US, UK, Canada, EU nations, and much of Latin America) qualify for the Friendly Nations Visa, which grants permanent residency quickly (often within 6–8 months). After maintaining this residency for 5 years (or 3 years if from an Ibero-American country), naturalization is available. This is the most popular pathway for Western expatriates.

  2. Pensionado Visa → Permanent Residency → Naturalization: Retirees receiving a pension of at least $1,000/month qualify for this famous program, which grants permanent residency along with substantial discounts on hotels, flights, restaurants, and healthcare. After the standard residency period, naturalization is possible.

  3. Ibero-American Fast Track: Citizens of Spain and Latin American nations benefit from a 3-year residency requirement instead of 5 — a significant advantage for regional migrants.

  4. Canal Zone Strategic Value: Panama’s geographical position at the crossroads of the Americas, its USD-based economy, and its free trade zones (notably the Colón Free Zone) make it a premier second residency and second citizenship destination for businesspeople and investors.

Special Clauses & Programs

Pensionado Program (Law 6 of 1987)

One of the most celebrated retirement visa programs in the world. Requirements:

  • Proof of a lifetime pension or annuity of at least $1,000/month (or $750/month for former Canal Zone employees)
  • No minimum investment required
  • Grants permanent residency immediately
  • Major discounts: 50% off entertainment, 30% off hotels, 25% off airlines, 15% off hospital bills, 10% off prescription medicines

Friendly Nations Visa (Executive Decree 343 of 2012, amended)

Available to nationals of countries with friendly ties to Panama (the list has varied but historically included 50+ countries including the US, Canada, UK, most of the EU, Australia, Japan, South Korea, and more). Requirements:

  • Proof of economic ties (employment, professional license, or business ownership in Panama)
  • Clean criminal record
  • Grants permanent residency — one of the fastest paths to PR in the region

Economic Solvency Visa

  • Minimum investment of $300,000 in Panamanian real estate, bank deposit, or business
  • Grants immediate permanent residency
  • Counts toward naturalization timeline

Self-Economic Solvency Visa

  • Minimum $300,000 in a fixed-term Panamanian bank deposit
  • Grants permanent residency

Reforestation Investor Visa

  • Minimum $80,000 investment in a registered reforestation project
  • Permanent residency
  • Tax incentives on reforestation income

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Panama does not have a formal citizenship-by-investment program. Citizenship can only be obtained through naturalization, which requires genuine residence in Panama for the statutory period.

All residency-by-investment programs lead to permanent residency, not immediate citizenship. Citizenship remains contingent on completing the naturalization residency period.

Entrepreneur & Startup Visas

  • Friendly Nations Visa (professional/business): Easiest pathway for entrepreneurs — establish a Panamanian company or obtain professional license, apply for residency
  • City of Knowledge (Ciudad del Saber): Special economic zone near the former Canal Zone; technology, research, and education companies can locate here with special visa treatment for employees
  • Panama Pacifica: Former Howard Air Force Base converted to a special economic zone; companies here receive tax incentives and employees/investors can obtain residency

Panama’s territorial tax system is one of its most attractive features for international entrepreneurs and digital nomads — income earned outside Panama is simply not taxed, regardless of residency status.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

  • Panama City is one of Latin America’s top financial centers
  • 60+ licensed banks including Citibank, HSBC, Banistmo, Banco Nacional de Panamá
  • US dollar is the functional currency (no exchange risk with USD)
  • FATCA compliant; CRS participant
  • Banking for non-residents is possible but increasingly requires stronger documentation due to AML compliance
  • Panama has been on the FATF “grey list” intermittently — verify current status before major financial planning

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Colón Free Zone (CFZ): The largest free trade zone in the Western Hemisphere; no import/export taxes on goods in transit
  • Panama Pacifico Special Economic Zone: Technology, logistics, aviation, and medical services — up to 15-year tax exemptions
  • City of Knowledge: R&D, education, and technology hub — special tax treatment and work visas
  • Tourism Incentives Law (Law 80 of 2012): Tax exemptions for approved tourism projects
  • Agriculture Incentives: Exemptions for agricultural export enterprises

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

A Panamanian national may voluntarily renounce Panamanian citizenship by:

  • Formal declaration before a Panamanian consular or civil authority
  • Submission of a written renunciation to the Civil Registry
  • Process is irrevocable upon completion

Involuntary Loss

CauseApplicable?Notes
Acquisition of a foreign nationalityNoDual nationality fully permitted since 1972 Constitution
Naturalization fraud / misrepresentationYesRevocation of naturalization decree
Service in a foreign military (enemy state)PossiblePresidential decree authority under wartime provisions
Taking oath of allegiance to a foreign power incompatible with Panamanian allegianceRare — discretionary

Panama-born nationals (jus soli or jus sanguinis) cannot be stripped of nationality in ordinary circumstances. The Constitution protects Panamanians from statelessness.

Recovery / Resumption

A former Panamanian national who renounced citizenship may petition for resumption of nationality through the Civil Registry and the Ministry of Government, provided they re-establish residency or demonstrate compelling ties to Panama.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed (Naturalization Application)

  • Valid passport (original + certified copies)
  • Panamanian permanent residency card (carnet de residente permanente)
  • Proof of continuous residence (entry/exit records, utility bills, lease agreements, bank statements)
  • Certificate of no criminal record — Panama (from National Police / PTJ)
  • Certificate of no criminal record — country of origin (apostilled)
  • Spanish language proficiency certificate (if applicable — from Ministry of Education or recognized institution)
  • Proof of financial solvency (employment letter, bank statements, business registration)
  • Birth certificate (apostilled + Spanish translation by certified translator)
  • Marriage certificate if applicable (apostilled + translation)
  • 4 recent passport-sized photographs (white background)
  • Application form (from Ministry of Government)
  • Application fee payment receipt
