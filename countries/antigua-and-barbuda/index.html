Dual Citizenship

Antigua and Barbuda fully permits dual and multiple citizenship. There is no requirement to renounce a prior nationality either upon naturalization through residency or upon obtaining citizenship through the Citizenship by Investment (CBI) program. Antiguans who acquire foreign nationality abroad are similarly not compelled to surrender their Antiguan passport.

The Antiguan passport is among the most powerful in the Caribbean. It provides visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to approximately 152 destinations worldwide, including all 27 Schengen Area member states, the United Kingdom, Ireland, and the majority of the Commonwealth. For investors from countries with weaker travel documents — particularly nationals of emerging market economies — the Antiguan passport represents a meaningful upgrade in global mobility.

Antigua and Barbuda is a member of the Commonwealth of Nations, the Caribbean Community (CARICOM), and the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS). CARICOM membership gives Antiguan citizens the right to seek employment in most other CARICOM member states without a work permit — a benefit relevant to regional mobility that goes beyond simple visa-free travel counts.

The Citizenship by Investment Program, established in 2013 under the Antigua and Barbuda Citizenship by Investment Act, is regulated by the Citizenship by Investment Unit (CIU) and is widely regarded as one of the top three Caribbean CBI programs globally alongside Dominica and St. Kitts and Nevis.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

A person born in Antigua and Barbuda acquires citizenship at birth if at least one parent is a citizen of Antigua and Barbuda or is lawfully and ordinarily resident in the country at the time of birth. Children born in Antigua and Barbuda to two non-citizen parents who are not lawfully resident do not automatically acquire citizenship. Children born on the islands to parents of unknown nationality may be treated as citizens for administrative purposes.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Citizenship by descent is available to persons born outside Antigua and Barbuda to a citizen parent. The registration process should be completed promptly after birth; the relevant application is filed with the Citizenship by Investment Unit or the Ministry responsible for immigration. Second-generation claims and beyond are more complex and may require specific registration within defined time windows. The law distinguishes between children born in wedlock (where citizenship flows through either parent) and those born outside wedlock (where citizenship historically flowed through the mother).

By Adoption

A child lawfully adopted by an Antiguan citizen may be registered as a citizen. The adoption must be recognized under Antiguan law, and the application is filed with the relevant immigration authority. Processing time is generally several months pending background checks and confirmation of the adoption’s legal validity.

By Marriage

Marriage to an Antiguan citizen does not confer automatic citizenship. A foreign spouse may apply for naturalization after completing a period of lawful residency. Under standard provisions, the spousal residency requirement may be shorter than the standard seven years, though this is subject to ministerial discretion. The marriage must be genuine, and applicants must demonstrate good character and ties to the country.

By Naturalization (Residency)

Standard naturalization requires seven years of lawful ordinary residence. Requirements include:

  • Seven years of lawful residence (continuous, with permitted absences)
  • Good character (police clearances from Antigua and Barbuda and all countries of prior residence)
  • Oath of allegiance to Antigua and Barbuda
  • Demonstrated intention to continue residing in the country
  • Basic English language ability (the country is English-speaking)

No formal language test or civics exam is required, though applicants may be interviewed by the immigration authorities. Applications are processed by the Ministry of Finance and Corporate Governance, which oversees the CIU and immigration functions. Processing for standard naturalization typically takes 12 to 24 months.

Citizenship / Residency by Investment

Overview

Antigua and Barbuda’s Citizenship by Investment (CBI) Program was established by the Antigua and Barbuda Citizenship by Investment Act, 2012, and came into operation on 1 November 2013. It is administered by the Citizenship by Investment Unit (CIU), under the authority of the Prime Minister’s office and the Ministry of Finance. The program is one of the most established and reputable in the Caribbean, with a robust four-tier due diligence process and a track record of thousands of approved applicants across more than a decade of operation.

Key advantages:

  • No prior residence required
  • No interview required
  • No language test
  • Full family inclusion
  • Dual citizenship permitted
  • Schengen + UK visa-free travel
  • Processing in as little as 3 months

Approved real estate projects are listed by the CIU and include luxury hotel shares, branded residential developments, and approved commercial properties. Joint purchase between two investors is permitted provided each contributes a minimum of USD $400,000 (effectively a USD $400,000/person threshold when shared, though the published minimum for individual investment remains USD $200,000 per approved project rules). Investors must retain the property for at least five years.

Note: Real estate investment does not typically produce rental income for the investor during the holding period unless specifically structured otherwise within the approved development.

Business investment must be in an eligible Antiguan business, creating employment and contributing to the national economy. The business must be approved by the CIU prior to application. This route is the least common due to higher capital requirements and greater documentation burden, but it offers the advantage of an operational business in a favorable tax jurisdiction.

5-Day Visit Requirement

A condition unique to the Antigua and Barbuda CBI program is the five-day physical visit requirement. Citizens who obtained nationality through the CBI program must spend at least five days in Antigua and Barbuda within the first five years of holding citizenship. Failure to satisfy this requirement may result in the loss of citizenship.

This requirement is enforced to demonstrate at least a nominal connection to the country. In practice, the five-day requirement is easily satisfied by a short vacation or business visit. Many CBI holders fulfill it at the time of passport collection or passport renewal. The Antiguan government has enforced this provision and revoked passports from holders who demonstrably had no connection to the country.

Due Diligence Process

The Antiguan CBI program operates a four-tier due diligence process:

  1. Authorized agent review: The licensed agent conducts initial screening and document verification
  2. CIU internal review: The CIU conducts its own independent due diligence using international databases
  3. Third-party due diligence firm: An independent third-party agency (typically a global risk consultancy) conducts background checks
  4. Security clearance: Final review by Antiguan security and intelligence services

All adult dependents (18+) are subject to the same level of due diligence as the main applicant. Children aged 12–17 undergo a lighter-touch review. The program only accepts applications submitted through a registered and licensed authorized agent — direct applications are not permitted.

The Antiguan passport does not provide visa-free access to the United States, Canada, or Australia. These are the most commonly cited limitations for investors from the US or those seeking US travel facilitation. However, the passport provides excellent coverage for travel to Europe, Asia, and the rest of the Americas.

Recent Developments

  • The CIU has been subject to reforms and increased international scrutiny following broader Caribbean CBI transparency debates, particularly involving pressure from the European Union and the United States Department of State
  • In 2022, the EU indicated it may restrict visa-free access to Schengen for citizens of certain Caribbean CBI countries if due diligence standards were not strengthened — Antigua responded with enhanced vetting procedures
  • The Antiguan government has blacklisted certain nationalities from the CBI program in response to geopolitical sanctions
  • In 2024, Antigua confirmed it maintains its full Schengen visa-free access, a key selling point of the program

Business & Entrepreneurship

Company Formation

Antigua and Barbuda offers two primary corporate vehicles for international business:

  • International Business Corporation (IBC): Exempt from local taxation; cannot do business within Antigua domestically; flexible structure; fast incorporation (24–72 hours)
  • Domestic Company: Subject to local tax and regulatory requirements; required for businesses operating within the country

IBCs are governed by the International Business Corporations Act. They require a registered agent and registered office in Antigua, but directors and shareholders may be non-resident.

Antigua is not a pure zero-tax jurisdiction for domestic operations, but IBCs conducting business exclusively outside Antigua are exempt from all Antiguan taxes. The country has signed a number of TIEAs and has committed to CRS/AEOI standards.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

The Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB) governs monetary policy across Antigua and the OECS. The Eastern Caribbean Dollar (XCD) is pegged to the USD at XCD 2.70 = USD 1.00, providing monetary stability. The financial sector includes domestic commercial banks, offshore banking facilities, and an emerging fintech sector. Antigua hosts the headquarters of the ECCB.

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Fiscal Incentives Act: Tax holidays for approved manufacturing and tourism investments (up to 15 years)
  • Hotel Aid Act: Duty-free importation of construction materials for approved hotel projects
  • Free Trade Zone: Designated zones for export-oriented manufacturing
  • Digital Economy initiatives: The government has expressed interest in attracting tech companies and remote workers

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

An Antiguan citizen of full age may renounce citizenship by making a formal declaration to the Minister responsible for citizenship. Renunciation may be withheld if the country is at war or if the renunciation is deemed contrary to public interest. A person who renounces Antiguan citizenship may lose the right to reside in Antigua without a separate visa or permit.

Involuntary Loss

CBI citizenship may be revoked if it was obtained through fraud, misrepresentation, or concealment of material facts. Citizenship may also be revoked from naturalized citizens who engage in treasonous conduct or serve in the armed forces of a foreign state at war with Antigua and Barbuda. Citizenship by birth is much more difficult to revoke. Failure to satisfy the five-day visit requirement within the first five years is a specific ground for revocation of CBI citizenship.

Recovery / Resumption

Former Antiguan citizens who lost citizenship through voluntary renunciation may apply to resume citizenship through application to the Minister. Resumption is discretionary and not guaranteed. Former CBI citizens who had citizenship revoked for fraud are generally not eligible for resumption.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

For the CBI program:

  • Completed application forms (provided by authorized agent)
  • Valid passport (all pages, all passports held in the past 10 years)
  • Birth certificate (certified/apostilled)
  • Marriage certificate if applicable
  • Police clearances from all countries of residence in the past 10 years
  • Bank reference letter and proof of funds (source of wealth documentation)
  • Certified net worth statement or audited financial statements
  • Physician’s certificate (medical history)
  • Passport-size photographs (biometric standard)
  • Proof of investment (NDF receipt, real estate purchase agreement, etc.)
  • Character references
  • CV / curriculum vitae

Where to Submit

All applications must be submitted through a licensed authorized agent registered with the CIU. The CIU does not accept direct applications from investors.

Citizenship by Investment Unit Ministry of Finance & Corporate Governance Government of Antigua and Barbuda St. John’s, Antigua

CIU website: https://cip.gov.ag

Processing Time

  • Standard processing: 3–6 months from submission of complete application
  • Expedited processing: Not officially available, though some agents claim priority review pathways
  • Physical presence in Antigua is not required at any stage except for the post-approval passport collection (which may also be done through the embassy in some cases)

Fees

See Investment Options section above for full fee schedule. Budget approximately USD $150,000 total for a single applicant via NDF and approximately USD $200,000–$210,000 for a family of four via NDF, inclusive of all government fees and a mid-range agent fee.
