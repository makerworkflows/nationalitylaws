Dual Citizenship — Not Permitted

Myanmar does not recognize or permit dual citizenship under any circumstance. The 1982 Citizenship Law is explicit: a citizen of Myanmar who acquires the citizenship of another country automatically ceases to be a citizen of Myanmar. Conversely, a foreign national seeking Myanmar citizenship must renounce all other nationalities before or upon naturalization.

This prohibition is strictly enforced. Myanmar nationals residing abroad who naturalize in another country are considered to have lost Myanmar citizenship. There is no retention mechanism or exception for dual nationals.

The prohibition extends to all three tiers of Myanmar’s citizenship system — full citizens, associate citizens, and naturalized citizens.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Sanguinis)

Myanmar does not apply jus soli. Birth on Myanmar territory alone does not confer citizenship. A child acquires full citizenship at birth only if both parents are full citizens of Myanmar. The system is strictly descent-based.

Three-tiered citizenship structure:

The 1982 Citizenship Law created three classes of citizenship, each with different rights:

  1. Full Citizens — Persons belonging to one of the 135 officially recognized “national races” (Bamar, Shan, Karen, Kachin, Chin, Kayah, Mon, Rakhine, and subgroups), or persons whose ancestors settled in Myanmar prior to 1823 (before the first Anglo-Burmese War). Full citizens enjoy all political rights, including the right to hold any government office.
  2. Associate Citizens — Persons who held citizenship under the 1948 Union Citizenship Act and applied under the 1982 law. Associate citizens have most rights of full citizens but cannot hold certain senior government positions.
  3. Naturalized Citizens — Foreign nationals who meet the naturalization requirements. Naturalized citizens have the most restricted rights, including prohibitions on holding the office of President, Vice President, Speaker of Parliament, Chief Justice, Commander-in-Chief, and other senior positions.

The distinction between these three tiers creates a de facto caste system within Myanmar’s citizenship framework. The classification of a person’s citizenship tier is recorded on their National Registration Card (NRC), which uses a color-coding system: pink for full citizens, blue for associate citizens, and green for naturalized citizens.

By Descent

A child born to two full citizens is a full citizen regardless of place of birth. A child born to one full citizen parent and one non-citizen parent may be eligible for associate citizenship but not full citizenship. Children of associate citizens or naturalized citizens inherit their parents’ tier or lower.

By Adoption

Myanmar law does not provide for automatic acquisition of citizenship through adoption. An adopted child may be eligible for naturalized citizenship if they meet the standard requirements.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Myanmar citizen does not confer citizenship. A foreign spouse must apply for naturalized citizenship through the standard process. There is no reduced residency period or spousal pathway.

Loss and Renunciation

Voluntary Renunciation

A Myanmar citizen may renounce citizenship by application if they have acquired or are about to acquire the citizenship of another country. The renunciation takes effect upon approval by the Central Body (Council of Ministers).

Applications are submitted through the Township Immigration Office to the Ministry of Immigration and Population, which forwards them to the Central Body. There is no fixed processing time and no fee schedule is publicly available.

Involuntary Loss

  • Acquisition of foreign citizenship: Automatic loss of Myanmar citizenship upon voluntarily acquiring another nationality. The person is expected to surrender their Myanmar identity documents.
  • Revocation: The government may revoke the citizenship of naturalized citizens and associate citizens for disloyalty, trading with the enemy, or fraud in the naturalization process. The Central Body has broad discretionary power in making this determination.
  • Prolonged absence: Naturalized citizens who reside abroad for extended periods without permission may have their citizenship revoked.
  • National security grounds: The 1982 law grants the government power to revoke citizenship in cases deemed threatening to state security, with no independent judicial review mechanism.

Restoration of Nationality

Myanmar law provides limited mechanisms for restoration. A former citizen who lost citizenship by acquiring a foreign nationality may apply for re-admission to citizenship, but this is discretionary and subject to the approval of the Central Body. The applicant must typically renounce their foreign nationality and return to reside in Myanmar.

In practice, restoration is rarely granted and the process is opaque. The political environment since the 2021 military coup has further complicated all citizenship-related proceedings.

Stateless Persons and Foundlings

Myanmar’s citizenship framework has been widely criticized by international human rights organizations for producing statelessness. The Rohingya population, numbering over one million, is the most prominent example — they are not recognized among the 135 “national races” and are effectively excluded from citizenship under the 1982 law. Despite having lived in Rakhine State for generations, most Rohingya are classified as “Bengali” foreigners and denied all citizenship tiers.

Other stateless populations include ethnic Chinese and Indian communities whose ancestors settled in Myanmar during the British colonial period but who have been unable to prove residence prior to 1823.

Foundlings (children of unknown parentage found in Myanmar) may be granted citizenship if both parents are unknown, but this is discretionary and not guaranteed by statute. There is no constitutional right to citizenship for foundlings.

Myanmar is not a party to the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons or the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness. The country has not cooperated meaningfully with UNHCR on statelessness reduction since the military takeover in 2021.

Document Checklist

  • Application form (obtained from the Township Immigration Office)
  • National Registration Card (NRC) or proof of prior citizenship status
  • Birth certificate
  • Household registration certificate (Form 66/6)
  • Two passport-sized photographs
  • Proof of residency in Myanmar (township records)
  • Character references from local authorities
  • Proof of language proficiency
  • Renunciation of foreign nationality (if applicable)
  • Marriage certificate (if applicable)
  • Evidence of livelihood and economic self-sufficiency
