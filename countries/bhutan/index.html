Dual Citizenship

Bhutan does not permit dual citizenship under any circumstance. This is enshrined in both the Citizenship Act of 1985 and the Constitution of Bhutan (2008).

Article 6(3) of the Constitution states: “A citizen of Bhutan shall not be a citizen of a foreign country.” This is an absolute prohibition with no exceptions.

Consequences:

  • A Bhutanese citizen who acquires foreign nationality automatically loses Bhutanese citizenship.
  • A foreign national applying for Bhutanese citizenship must renounce all other nationalities before naturalization can be granted.
  • There is no provision for retention of Bhutanese citizenship while holding another nationality, even temporarily.

This reflects Bhutan’s emphasis on maintaining a cohesive national identity and cultural sovereignty as a small Himalayan kingdom.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Bhutan does not recognize jus soli (citizenship by birth on territory). Being born in Bhutan does not confer citizenship. Citizenship is determined exclusively by parentage.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Under the Constitution and the 1985 Citizenship Act:

  1. Both parents Bhutanese: A person whose father and mother are both citizens of Bhutan is a citizen by birth (Article 6(1) of the Constitution).
  2. One parent Bhutanese: A person whose one parent is a citizen of Bhutan may be granted citizenship by naturalization, provided they have been registered and meet residency requirements. This is not automatic.

Historical context: Under the 1958 Citizenship Act, citizenship was transmitted primarily through the father. The 1985 Act tightened requirements significantly, requiring both parents to be citizens for automatic citizenship by descent, which affected the status of many southern Bhutanese of Nepali descent.

By Adoption

There is no specific provision in the Citizenship Act for automatic citizenship by adoption. Adopted children may apply for citizenship through naturalization, subject to all standard requirements.

By Marriage

Marriage to a Bhutanese citizen does not confer automatic citizenship. A foreign spouse must:

  1. Have been domiciled in Bhutan for at least 15 years after the registration of marriage.
  2. Have no record of having spoken or acted against the King, Country, and People of Bhutan.
  3. Formally renounce citizenship of any foreign state.
  4. Be granted citizenship at the discretion of the Druk Gyalpo (King).

By Naturalization

Naturalization requirements are among the most restrictive in Asia.

Loss and Renunciation

Voluntary Renunciation

A Bhutanese citizen may voluntarily renounce citizenship by submitting a written application to the Ministry of Home and Cultural Affairs. The renunciation must be accepted by the government.

Restoration of Nationality

There is no formal statutory procedure for restoration of Bhutanese citizenship. However, the Constitution provides:

Article 6(4): “A person who has lost citizenship of Bhutan may apply to resume citizenship” subject to conditions prescribed by law.

In practice, former citizens may petition the government through the Ministry of Home and Cultural Affairs. Restoration is discretionary and typically requires:

  1. Return to Bhutan and establishment of permanent residence.
  2. Renunciation of any foreign nationality held.
  3. Demonstration of continued cultural and national ties to Bhutan.
  4. Approval by the government.

Stateless Persons and Foundlings

Bhutan is not a party to the 1954 Convention on Statelessness or the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness.

  • Foundlings: There is no explicit statutory provision for foundlings. In practice, children found in Bhutan with unknown parents may be registered, but automatic citizenship is not guaranteed.
  • Statelessness concern: The 1985 Citizenship Act’s strict requirements (both parents must be citizens) contributed to large-scale statelessness among the Lhotshampa (southern Bhutanese of Nepali origin), leading to the displacement of over 100,000 people in the early 1990s. Many were resettled to third countries through UNHCR programs.

Document Checklist

  • Completed citizenship application form (from Department of Immigration)
  • Census record (Bhutanese census registration is critical for proving domicile)
  • Birth certificate or equivalent document
  • Parents’ citizenship identity cards (for descent-based claims)
  • Marriage certificate (if applying as a spouse)
  • Proof of 15 or 20 years of continuous domicile in Bhutan
  • Dzongkha language proficiency evidence
  • Police clearance certificate from Bhutanese authorities
  • Renunciation certificate of foreign nationality
  • Passport-sized photographs (4 copies)
  • Security clearance from Royal Bhutan Police
