Dual Citizenship

Yes — Canada has fully recognized dual (and multiple) citizenship since 1977; you are not required to renounce any other nationality when acquiring Canadian citizenship, and becoming a citizen of another country does not cause you to lose Canadian citizenship.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

Canada grants citizenship at birth to any person born on Canadian territory, regardless of the nationality of the parents. This is unconditional jus soli with only two exceptions:

  • Children born to foreign diplomats accredited to Canada who enjoy diplomatic immunity
  • Children born to employees of foreign governments who are not permanent residents

Children born in Canada acquire citizenship automatically and do not need to apply. A Canadian birth certificate serves as proof of citizenship, though a formal citizenship certificate can also be obtained.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Canadian citizenship is transmitted by descent, but with a first-generation limit introduced by the Citizenship Act amendments of April 17, 2009:

  • First generation born abroad: A child born outside Canada to a Canadian citizen parent who was themselves born in Canada (or naturalized in Canada) automatically acquires Canadian citizenship at birth. No application is required, but a citizenship certificate should be obtained.
  • Second generation born abroad: A child born outside Canada to a Canadian citizen parent who was themselves born outside Canada does NOT automatically acquire Canadian citizenship. This is the “first-generation limit” designed to prevent citizenship from being passed indefinitely to generations with no connection to Canada.

Exceptions to the first-generation limit:

  • Children born to or adopted by a Crown servant (Canadian government employee serving abroad) or a Canadian Forces member posted abroad
  • Children who would otherwise be stateless (under certain conditions)

Prior to April 17, 2009: Canadian citizenship could be transmitted without generational limit, though persons born abroad in the second or subsequent generation had to apply for retention by age 28 under earlier legislation.

By Adoption

A child adopted abroad by a Canadian citizen can obtain citizenship through two pathways:

  1. Direct citizenship grant (since 2007): The adopting Canadian parent can apply to grant citizenship directly to the adopted child without the child first becoming a permanent resident. The adoption must comply with the laws of the child’s country of origin and the receiving Canadian province/territory, and the Hague Convention on Intercountry Adoption where applicable.
  2. Sponsorship for permanent residence: The child is sponsored as a permanent resident and subsequently applies for citizenship through the standard naturalization process.

The first-generation limit also applies to adopted children: a Canadian citizen who was themselves born abroad and acquired citizenship by descent cannot pass citizenship directly to an adopted child born abroad.

By Marriage

There is no special pathway to Canadian citizenship through marriage. Marrying a Canadian citizen does not automatically confer citizenship or permanent residence. A foreign spouse must:

  1. First obtain permanent residence (typically through spousal sponsorship, which takes approximately 12 months)
  2. Then meet the standard naturalization requirements, including 1,095 days of physical presence within 5 years

The only advantage marriage provides is access to the spousal sponsorship immigration stream, which is one of the faster pathways to permanent residence.

Physical presence calculation:

  • Each day of physical presence as a permanent resident counts as one full day
  • Each day of physical presence as a temporary resident or protected person before becoming a permanent resident counts as half a day, up to a maximum credit of 365 days
  • Applicants must have been physically present for at least 1,095 days during the 5 years immediately before the date of application

Language requirement details:

  • Canadian Language Benchmarks (CLB) Level 4 or Niveaux de competence linguistique canadiens (NCLC) Level 4
  • Tests listening and speaking ability (reading and writing not required for citizenship)
  • Accepted evidence: government-funded language training results, completion of secondary or post-secondary education in English or French, or third-party test results (IELTS, CELPIP, TEF, TCF)

Citizenship test:

  • 20 questions, multiple choice and true/false
  • 30 minutes to complete
  • Must score at least 15/20 (75%)
  • Based on the study guide “Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship”
  • Covers Canadian history, geography, government, rights and responsibilities, and symbols

Special Clauses & Programs

Lost Canadians

“Lost Canadians” refers to individuals who were or should have been Canadian citizens but lost their citizenship (or never acquired it) due to provisions in previous citizenship legislation. Several rounds of legislative amendments have restored citizenship to these groups:

2009 Amendments (Bill C-37):

  • Restored citizenship to most persons who lost it under the 1947 Canadian Citizenship Act
  • Key groups restored: persons born abroad to a Canadian parent before February 15, 1977, who did not apply for retention; persons who lost citizenship by acquiring another nationality before 1977; “war brides” and their children; persons born in Canada before 1947 who were British subjects
  • Applied automatically on April 17, 2009 — no application needed

2015 Amendments (Bill C-24 and Bill C-6):

  • Extended restoration to additional groups, including persons born abroad in the second or subsequent generation before April 17, 2009
  • Persons born between January 1, 1947 and April 16, 2009 to a Canadian parent in the first generation born abroad are citizens automatically

Who may still be a “Lost Canadian”:

  • Persons who renounced citizenship formally and have not reapplied
  • Persons born abroad in the second or subsequent generation after April 17, 2009 (caught by the first-generation limit)
  • Certain persons whose parents lost citizenship before their birth

If you believe you may be a Lost Canadian, you can apply for a search of citizenship records and, if applicable, a citizenship certificate confirming your status.

Start-Up Visa Program

Canada’s Start-Up Visa Program provides a pathway to permanent residence (and eventually citizenship) for immigrant entrepreneurs. Requirements:

  • A qualifying business supported by a designated venture capital fund (minimum $200,000 investment), angel investor group ($75,000), or business incubator
  • CLB 5 in English or French
  • Sufficient settlement funds
  • The program grants permanent residence, after which standard citizenship timelines apply

Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)

Each Canadian province and territory operates its own immigration nomination program, which can accelerate permanent residence and thereby the pathway to citizenship. These are not direct citizenship programs but facilitate faster PR processing for candidates with skills or connections valued by specific provinces.

Canadian Armed Forces Fast-Track

Members of the Canadian Armed Forces (including non-citizens who are permanent residents) may have reduced residency requirements. Time spent serving abroad on military postings counts toward physical presence requirements.

Investment Pathways

Citizenship by Investment (CBI)

Canada does not offer direct citizenship by investment. There is no program that grants Canadian citizenship in exchange for a financial contribution. All investors must first obtain permanent residence and then satisfy standard naturalization requirements.

Golden Visa / Residency by Investment (RBI)

Canada’s investment immigration landscape has evolved significantly:

Start-Up Visa (Active):

  • Not a passive investment program; requires active involvement in an innovative business
  • Must secure support from a designated organization (VC fund, angel group, or incubator)
  • Leads to permanent residence, not direct citizenship
  • Processing time: 12-16 months (varies)

Quebec Immigrant Investor Program:

  • Historically the primary investment immigration pathway
  • Has been suspended and reopened multiple times; currently suspended indefinitely since 2019
  • When active: required net worth of CAD $2 million and a CAD $1.2 million investment (interest-free loan to Quebec government for 5 years)

Provincial Programs:

  • Some provinces have operated entrepreneur or investor streams through their PNPs
  • Requirements and availability vary by province
  • Generally require active business ownership/management rather than passive investment

Owner-Operator LMIA Pathway (Informal):

  • Some immigration consultants facilitate purchasing or establishing a Canadian business
  • Business owner obtains a work permit via Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA)
  • After operating the business and obtaining PR through Express Entry or PNP, standard citizenship path applies
  • Not a formal government program; relies on standard immigration streams

Business & Entrepreneurship

Canada consistently ranks among the best countries in the world for entrepreneurs, combining strong immigration pathways specifically designed for founders with a well-funded startup ecosystem and favorable tax treatment for small businesses.

Formation costCAD $200 (federal) / varies by province

Online registrationYes — Corporations Canada

Canada offers several business structures: sole proprietorship, partnership, federal corporation, and provincial corporation. For startups, incorporation is most common — federal incorporation allows you to operate in any province under one name, while provincial is cheaper and simpler if you only need one province. No minimum share capital is required. You can also register a cooperative for community-focused ventures.

Foreign nationals can incorporate a Canadian company, but at least 25% of directors must be Canadian residents (some provinces require 50%).

Entrepreneur & Startup Visas

Start-Up Visa Program (SUV) — Canada’s flagship immigration pathway for entrepreneurs:

  • Requires a letter of support from a designated organization (angel investor group, venture capital fund, or business incubator)
  • No minimum personal investment required
  • Grants permanent residence directly (not a temporary visa first)
  • Processing time: 12-16 months
  • Applicants can get a work permit to enter Canada while the PR application processes
  • Up to 5 founders per startup can apply

Other pathways:

  • Provincial Nominee Program (PNP) — Entrepreneur streams: BC, Ontario, Manitoba, and Saskatchewan offer entrepreneur-specific immigration. Requirements vary (typically CAD $200K-600K investment, must create jobs)
  • Self-employed visa: For individuals with relevant experience in cultural activities, athletics, or farm management
  • Intra-Company Transfer (ICT): If you have an existing business outside Canada, you can transfer to a Canadian branch

Canada’s small business deduction is a major incentive: the first CAD $500,000 of active business income is taxed at just 9% federally (combined rate as low as 11% in some provinces). The Scientific Research & Experimental Development (SR&ED) program offers tax credits of 15-35% on qualifying R&D expenditures — one of the most generous R&D incentive programs globally.

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

Foreign entrepreneurs can open a Canadian business bank account, though most banks require in-person verification. Major banks serving startups include RBC, TD, and BMO, with fintech alternatives like Wealthsimple, Wise, and Neo Financial growing rapidly.

  • Stripe is fully available in Canada
  • No foreign exchange controls or capital movement restrictions
  • Canada has a mature payments infrastructure with Interac e-Transfer for domestic payments
  • International wire transfers are straightforward through any major bank

  • Communitech (Waterloo) — deep tech and AI-focused accelerator ecosystem
  • Creative Destruction Lab (CDL) — science and technology seed-stage program with global reach
  • BDC (Business Development Bank of Canada) — government-backed startup loans and VC fund

  • Industrial Research Assistance Program (IRAP) — advisory services and funding for tech SMEs
  • Digital Technology Supercluster (Vancouver) — collaborative R&D projects
  • Provincial accelerators: DMZ (Toronto), Platform Calgary, Volta (Halifax), District 3 (Montreal)

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

Canadian citizens may voluntarily renounce their citizenship by applying to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). Requirements:

  • Must be a citizen of another country or will become a citizen of another country upon renunciation
  • Must be 18 years of age or older (or authorized by a parent/guardian)
  • Must not be subject to a revocation proceeding
  • Must not reside in Canada (renunciation from within Canada requires demonstrating that a security, criminal, or human rights ground does not apply)
  • Processing fee: CAD $100
  • Processing time: approximately 6-12 months

Involuntary Loss

Canadian citizenship can only be revoked in very limited circumstances:

Revocation for fraud:

  • If citizenship was obtained by fraud, false representation, or knowingly concealing material circumstances
  • IRCC can initiate revocation proceedings
  • The affected person has the right to a hearing before the Federal Court (for serious cases) or to make written submissions to the Minister’s delegate

Revocation does NOT occur for:

  • Acquiring another citizenship (since 1977, dual citizenship is fully recognized)
  • Residing permanently outside Canada
  • Failure to maintain ties to Canada
  • Marriage to a foreign national

Note: Between 2014 and 2017 (under Bill C-24), Canadian citizenship could be revoked from dual nationals convicted of terrorism, treason, or espionage. Bill C-6 (2017) repealed these provisions. Canadian citizenship can no longer be revoked on national security grounds.

Recovery / Resumption

Former Canadian citizens who renounced or lost citizenship can reacquire it by:

  1. Resumption of citizenship: Persons who lost citizenship under the pre-1977 Act (and were not automatically restored by the 2009 amendments) may apply for a grant of citizenship. They must first become permanent residents and meet standard requirements.
  2. Standard naturalization: Former citizens who voluntarily renounced must first regain permanent resident status and then apply for citizenship through the normal naturalization process (1,095 days physical presence in 5 years).
  3. Automatic restoration: As noted under “Lost Canadians,” many former citizens were automatically restored by the 2009 and 2015 legislative amendments.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Completed Application for Canadian Citizenship (CIT 0002 for adults, CIT 0003 for minors)
  • Photocopy of Permanent Resident Card (front and back)
  • Photocopies of two pieces of personal identification (one with photo)
  • Two Canadian citizenship photos meeting specifications (53mm x 35mm)
  • Proof of language proficiency (accepted test results, transcripts, or training certificates) — ages 18-54
  • Photocopies of Canadian tax returns (Notices of Assessment or T4s) for 3 of the 5 years in the physical presence period
  • Physical presence calculator (online tool provided by IRCC)
  • Photocopy of all pages of all passports and travel documents held during the 5-year period
  • Proof of identity for name change (if applicable — marriage certificate, legal name change document)
  • Certified translations of any documents not in English or French
  • Application fee payment receipt (CAD $630 adult / CAD $100 minor)
  • Supplementary documents if applicable: military service records, evidence of Crown servant status, proof of statelessness

Where to Submit

From inside Canada:

  • Applications are submitted by mail to the Case Processing Centre in Sydney, Nova Scotia
  • Address: Case Processing Centre — Sydney, P.O. Box 7000, Sydney, NS B1P 6V6
  • Some applicants may be eligible for online submission through the IRCC portal
  • Citizenship test and ceremony are conducted in person at IRCC offices across Canada

From abroad:

  • Canadian citizenship applications can only be submitted while physically residing in Canada (you must have met the physical presence requirement)
  • Persons residing abroad cannot apply; they must return to Canada
  • Exception: Canadian Armed Forces members stationed abroad may apply through military channels

Online:

  • IRCC has progressively expanded online application capabilities
  • Check the IRCC website for current online application availability
  • The physical presence calculator is available online
  • Status tracking is available through the IRCC online portal
