Dual Citizenship

Yes — Portugal fully allows dual and multiple citizenship. No renunciation of any foreign nationality is required when acquiring Portuguese citizenship, and Portuguese citizens do not lose their nationality by acquiring another.

Acquisition of Nationality

By Birth (Jus Soli)

All persons born in Portuguese territory (including Macau) before October 3, 1981 automatically received citizenship at birth regardless of parents’ nationalities.

Since 1981, persons born in Portugal receive citizenship at birth if at least one parent is a Portuguese citizen.

Stateless children born in Portugal automatically become citizens with no further requirements.

By Descent (Jus Sanguinis)

Individuals born overseas to a Portuguese parent are eligible to acquire citizenship by descent if their birth is registered at a Portuguese diplomatic mission, or if they register as citizens at any later point.

Grandchildren of Portuguese citizens may also acquire citizenship by descent with additional requirements:

  • Formally declare intention of becoming Portuguese
  • Prove substantial ties with Portugal
  • Register birth in the national registry

By Adoption

Adopted children of Portuguese citizens automatically acquire citizenship on completion of the adoption process.

One Year Residency Requirement (Children of Non-Citizens)

Children born in Portugal to non-citizens are eligible to declare intent to acquire Portuguese citizenship if at least one parent has been resident in Portugal for a minimum of 1 year (legal or otherwise). This is current as of 2024.

Historical changes:

  • 1981-1994: Parent needed 6 years of residency
  • 1994-2006: 10 years for non-Lusophone parents
  • 2006-present: 5 years for any foreign parent
  • 2024-present: 1 year for any parent

By Marriage

Individuals married to Portuguese citizens may acquire citizenship by declaration after 3 years of marriage. No residency in Portugal is required.

By Naturalization (Residency)

General requirement: 5 years of legal residency

Additional requirements:

  • Language: Portuguese, A2 level (basic)
  • Criminal record: No conviction resulting in imprisonment for more than 3 years
  • No threat to national defense

Noncitizen minors born in Portugal who meet language and behavior requirements are eligible for naturalization if they have completed at least one cycle of domestic primary or secondary education and have at least one parent who has resided in Portugal for the previous 5 years.

Special Clauses & Programs

Sephardic Jewish Law of Return

Status: Active (with increased scrutiny since March 2022) Legal basis: Decree-Law 43/2013, amended by Decree-Law 30-A/2015 Effective dates: March 1, 2015 — ongoing

Portugal grants citizenship to descendants of Portuguese Sephardic Jews expelled during the Inquisition. No residency requirement.

Requirements:

  • Prove membership in a Sephardic community of Portuguese origin with ties to Portugal
  • Prove Sephardic lineage in family tree
  • Language spoken at home (Ladino / Judeo-Portuguese / Judeo-Spanish is a factor)
  • Demonstrate “emotional and traditional connection” with the Portuguese Sephardic community — commonly via a letter from an Orthodox rabbi confirming Jewish heritage
  • Since March 2022: Must prove “effective connection with Portugal” (increased scrutiny after fraud cases)

Who certifies: Jewish Community of Lisbon (Lisbon and environs) or Jewish Community of Porto (Porto and northern Portugal)

Scale: As of January 2023, over 300,000 pending cases. By November 2020, ~23,000 granted out of ~76,000 applications.

Note: Portugal became the only country besides Israel enforcing a Jewish Law of Return when this passed. Spain adopted a similar measure two years later.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Portugal has transformed itself into one of Europe’s most attractive destinations for entrepreneurs, combining dedicated startup visa programs, a favorable tax regime for new residents, low cost of living compared to Western Europe, and a thriving tech ecosystem centered in Lisbon and Porto.

Company Formation

Most common entity typeLda. (Sociedade por Quotas — limited liability)
Formation timeline1-2 business days (Empresa na Hora)
Formation costEUR 360 (Empresa na Hora one-stop shop)
Minimum capitalEUR 1 per partner (effectively none)
Online registrationYes — Empresa Online

Portugal’s Empresa na Hora (“Company in an Hour”) program is one of the fastest company formation processes in Europe. You can register an Lda. (the Portuguese equivalent of an LLC) at designated registry offices with pre-approved articles of association. Foreign nationals can be sole shareholders and directors with no residency requirement for ownership.

Other entity types include the SA (public limited company, minimum EUR 50K capital), sole proprietorship (Empresario em Nome Individual), and branch office of a foreign company.

Entrepreneur & Startup Visas

StartUP Visa — Portugal’s dedicated program for non-EU tech entrepreneurs:

  • Must have an innovative, technology-based, or knowledge-intensive business idea
  • Requires incubation agreement with a certified Portuguese incubator
  • No minimum investment requirement
  • Grants a 1-year residence permit, renewable for 2-year periods
  • Path to permanent residence after 5 years, citizenship after 5+ years
  • Includes family reunification

Tech Visa — For hiring skilled workers at certified startups:

  • Companies must be IAPMEI-certified as tech/innovative
  • Streamlined work visa processing for non-EU employees
  • 120-day initial visa, convertible to residence permit

D2 Visa (Independent Worker/Entrepreneur):

  • For freelancers, self-employed, and business owners
  • Requires proof of income or business viability
  • Grants residence permit, renewable annually

Digital Nomad Visa (D8):

  • For remote workers earning from non-Portuguese sources
  • Minimum income: 4x Portuguese minimum wage (~EUR 3,400/month)
  • Favorable NHR tax treatment possible

Tax Overview

VAT23% standard (6-13% reduced rates)
Capital gains tax28% (or included in progressive income tax)
Personal income tax (top rate)48% + 5% solidarity surcharge

NHR regime20% flat rate on Portuguese-source employment income (10 years)

Portugal’s Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) tax regime was a major draw for entrepreneurs — offering a flat 20% tax on Portuguese-source professional income and potential exemptions on foreign-source income for 10 years. While the original NHR program closed to new applicants in 2024, a modified version (NHR 2.0 / Tax Incentive for Scientific Research and Innovation) continues for qualifying professionals in tech, R&D, and innovation roles.

The SME corporate tax rate of 17% on the first EUR 50,000 of taxable income makes Portugal competitive for early-stage startups. Portugal also offers SIFIDE II R&D tax credits (32.5-50% of qualifying R&D expenditure).

Banking & Financial Infrastructure

Opening a Portuguese business bank account is straightforward for foreigners with a valid NIF (tax identification number). You can obtain a NIF before arriving through a fiscal representative.

  • Major banks: Millennium BCP, Novo Banco, Caixa Geral de Depositos, ActivoBank (digital-first)
  • Stripe is fully available in Portugal
  • Fintech ecosystem: Revolut Business widely used, local fintechs like Coverflex and Rauva growing
  • No foreign exchange controls within the EU/EEA
  • SEPA transfers for instant European payments
  • Portugal has one of the highest contactless payment adoption rates in Europe

Key Programs & Incentives

  • Startup Portugal — government initiative providing ecosystem support, startup vouchers (EUR 75K), and the StartUP Visa program
  • IAPMEI — public agency supporting SMEs with financing, mentoring, and certification
  • Portugal Ventures — public VC fund investing in Portuguese startups (seed to Series A)
  • Web Summit — world’s largest tech conference, headquartered in Lisbon since 2016 (massive networking pipeline)
  • Station F Portugal Program — partnership with the Paris-based mega-campus
  • Beta-i (Lisbon) — leading accelerator and innovation consultancy
  • Startup Lisboa — free incubation space and mentoring in central Lisbon
  • Porto Digital — digital innovation hub and coworking ecosystem in Porto
  • Tec Labs / Building Global Innovators — MIT Portugal accelerator at University of Lisbon
  • Free Trade Zones: Madeira International Business Centre offers reduced 5% corporate tax rate for licensed companies
  • Portugal 2030: EUR 23B in EU structural funds, significant portion allocated to innovation and digital transformation

Loss of Nationality

Voluntary Renunciation

Portuguese citizenship can be relinquished by making a declaration of renunciation, provided the declarant already possesses another nationality. It cannot be renounced if it would make a person stateless.

Involuntary Loss

Portuguese citizenship cannot be involuntarily stripped by the government in any situation.

Where birth records were forged to acquire citizenship, the nullification is not treated as “loss” but as if the individual never acquired it.

Recovery / Resumption

Former citizens who lost citizenship while they were children may apply to reacquire. The Public Prosecution Service has one year to formally oppose, otherwise it is granted.

Women who lost Portuguese citizenship by marriage to foreign men can apply for reacquisition without opposition.

Any person who lost citizenship by acquiring another nationality automatically had Portuguese citizenship restored in 1981, unless that loss had been registered with the government.

Practical Guide

Documents Needed

  • Birth certificate (apostilled and translated to Portuguese)
  • Valid passport
  • Criminal background check (from country of origin and Portugal)
  • Proof of residency (5 years)
  • Portuguese language certificate (A2 level)
  • Proof of ties to Portugal (for Sephardic pathway)
  • Marriage certificate (for spousal pathway)
  • NIF (Portuguese tax number)

Where to Submit

  • Inside Portugal: Conservatoria dos Registos Centrais, Lisbon, or local civil registry offices
  • From abroad: Portuguese consulate/embassy in your country
  • Online portal: IRN - Instituto dos Registos e do Notariado

Processing Time

12-24 months for standard naturalization. Sephardic pathway can take significantly longer due to backlog (300,000+ pending cases as of 2023).
